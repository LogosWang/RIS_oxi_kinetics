\section{Mesh Sensitivity of the Segregation Profiles and the Finite-Efficiency Sink Condition}
\label{sec:si-sink}

Figure~\ref{fig:si-mesh} shows the Cr, Ni and Si profiles across the grain
boundary calculated at four grid spacings \(\Delta x\) with the two boundary
conditions for the point defects. With the perfect-sink (Dirichlet) condition
the solution does not converge under mesh refinement. As \(\Delta x\) is
reduced, the point-defect profiles steepen at the boundary, and both the
gradient and the magnitude of the solute segregation increase monotonically
with refinement, Fig.~\ref{fig:si-mesh}(a--c). The width of the segregation
profiles likewise fails to converge. With the finite-efficiency (Robin)
condition, Eqs.~\eqref{M-eq:sink-vac} and \eqref{M-eq:sink-int} of the main
text, the profiles converge under refinement, Fig.~\ref{fig:si-mesh}(d--f).

\section{Partition of the Unoxidized Metal at the Oxidation Front}
\label{sec:si-redistribution}

\subsection{Motivation}

If all the unoxidized metal swept by the advancing front were rejected to the
metal side of the interface, \(\lambda_i=0\) in
Eq.~\eqref{M-eq:capture-fraction} of the main text, the metal removed at the
front would be exactly the metal consumed by the three interfacial reactions.
Because the interfacial rate constant of Cr is much larger than that of Fe,
this limit depletes Cr and enriches Fe strongly in the metal at the interface.
The EDX line scans of \textcite{deng2017effect} show neither
(Fig.~\ref{M-fig:edx-compare} of the main text). In the irradiated specimens,
moreover, the Cr profile has a valley at the centre of the oxide that deepens
with dose, which shows that the interfacial Cr concentration follows the
radiation-induced segregation profile as the front advances and is not
modulated by the selective oxidation. The excess Fe and Ni must therefore
leave the interface by a path other than rejection into the metal.

\subsection{Limiting behaviour}

The recession rate of the interface, \(v_{\ox}\) of Eq.~\eqref{M-eq:vlat-ox}
of the main text, has two limits.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \(v_i^{\mathrm{r}}\to\infty\) for every element: \(\lambda_i=0\), the
        excess is rejected entirely into the metal and
        \(v_{\ox}=\sum_iJ_{\mathrm{r}}^{i}\); the metal removed is the metal
        oxidized.
  \item \(v_i^{\mathrm{r}}\ll v_{\lat}^{\ox,\max}\) for every element:
        \(\lambda_i\to1\) and the whole swept volume is removed. For every
        element with \(E_i>0\),
        \(\tilde{J}_{\mathrm{r}}^{i}=v_{\lat}^{\ox,\max}C_i^{\GB}\), and the
        same holds for the element that sets \(v_{\lat}^{\ox,\max}\), for which
        \(E_i=0\) and \(J_{\mathrm{r}}^{i}=v_{\lat}^{\ox,\max}C_i^{\GB}\).
        Since \(\sum_iC_i^{\GB}=1\),
        \begin{equation}
        v_{\ox}=\sum_i v_{\lat}^{\ox,\max}C_i^{\GB}=v_{\lat}^{\ox,\max}:
        \label{eq:si-vox-limit}
        \end{equation}
        the interface recedes at the sweep velocity, and the composition
        profile in the metal is translated rigidly towards the interface
        without modification by the selective oxidation.
\end{itemize}
Intermediate values give
\(\sum_iJ_{\mathrm{r}}^{i}\le v_{\ox}\le v_{\lat}^{\ox,\max}\), with the
partition set element by element.

\subsection{Relation to solute trapping}

Equation~\eqref{M-eq:capture-fraction} of the main text takes its form from
the continuous-growth partition coefficient \(k(v)\) of solute trapping quoted
there. In the solute-trapping theory of rapid solidification
\parencite{aziz1982model,aziz1988continuous} a solute atom swept by the
interface escapes into the parent phase only by a diffusive exchange jump of
length \(a\) with interface diffusivity \(D_i\), so that the diffusive
velocity is \(v_D=D_i/a\). The partition coefficient follows from the
competition between the advance of the interface by one monolayer, in a time
\(a/v\), and the escape, in a time \(a^{2}/D_i\); it goes from the equilibrium
value \(k_e\) for \(v\ll v_D\) to complete trapping, \(k=1\), for
\(v\gg v_D\). \textcite{aziz1988continuous} note that the model is not
specific to solidification and applies to solid-state transformations.
\textcite{yu2018nonequilibrium} and \textcite{sherman2019thermodynamics}
transposed it to the oxide/metal interface as nonequilibrium solute capture:
oxides form with compositions outside their equilibrium solubility limits when
(i) the captured oxide has a lower free energy than the alloy it forms from
and (ii) the effective velocity of cation incorporation exceeds the diffusive
velocity of cation exchange across the interface. They argue that for the
oxidation of Ni--Cr alloys condition (i) is met over essentially the whole
composition range, so that the oxide composition is set by the kinetics, and
that condition (ii) holds for most high-temperature and almost all aqueous
oxidation. The interface exchange diffusivities that would fix \(v_D\) are not
known for the Fe--Cr--Ni oxide/metal interface
\parencite{yu2018nonequilibrium}, so the rejection velocities
\(v_i^{\mathrm{r}}\) are treated as element-specific parameters; the element
that sets \(v_{\lat}^{\ox,\max}\) (Cr under the present conditions) has
\(E_i=0\) and is unaffected by its value.

\section{Cross-Section-Averaged Oxygen Transport and the Lateral Closure}
\label{sec:si-closure}

This section derives the one-dimensional oxygen balance,
Eqs.~\eqref{M-eq:O-mass} and \eqref{M-eq:O-flux} of the main text, and the
closure for the interfacial flux, Eqs.~\eqref{M-eq:film-transport} and
\eqref{M-eq:qss-closure} of the main text, from the two-dimensional diffusion
equation in the oxide cross-section, and gives the solution procedure for the
eigenvalue \(\mu\), the neglected higher modes and the range of validity.

\subsection{Notation}
\label{sec:notation}

The notation is that of the main text: \(y\) runs along the grain boundary
from the surface, \(x\) is normal to it with \(x=0\) on the mid-plane, and only
the half-domain \(0\le x\le L(y,t)\) is considered, with \(L\) the half-width
of Eq.~\eqref{M-eq:halfwidth} of the main text. \(\Dy\) and \(\Dx\) are the
effective diffusivities along and across the boundary,
Eq.~\eqref{M-eq:Deff} of the main text. \(C_{\Ox}(x,y,t)\) denotes the local
concentration of dissolved oxygen in the section; its cross-section average
and its value at the interface are
\begin{equation}
\Cb(y,t)=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L} C_{\Ox}\,\dd x,\qquad
\Ci=C_{\Ox}(L,y,t),
\label{eq:avg-def}
\end{equation}
and the diffusive oxygen flux reaching the interface, per unit area of
interface and positive towards the metal, is
\begin{equation}
\Jr(y,t)=-\Dx\,\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial x}\bigg|_{x=L}.
\label{eq:Jr-def}
\end{equation}
The interfacial reaction law is the total consumption
\(\Jr(\Ci)=\sum_k\Jr^{k}(\Ci)\) of the rate laws,
Eqs.~\eqref{M-eq:rate-cr2o3}--\eqref{M-eq:rate-sio2} of the main text; at
fixed metal concentrations it is a nondecreasing function of \(\Ci\).

\subsection{Assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[A1.] Equivalent homogeneous medium. The oxide, whose diffusivity
        \(D_{\Ox}(x)\) varies across the section, is replaced by a homogeneous
        anisotropic medium with diffusivity \(\Dy\) along the boundary and
        \(\Dx\) across it. The two means carry the same steady flux as the
        actual profile for a uniform gradient along the boundary and for a
        one-dimensional flux across the section, respectively.
  \item[A2.] Symmetry at the mid-plane: \(\partial C_{\Ox}/\partial x=0\) at
        \(x=0\).
  \item[A3.] Slow variation along the boundary:
        \(|\partial L/\partial y|\ll1\), and \(L\), \(\Dx\), \(\Dy\) and the
        metal concentrations that enter the reaction law change little over
        the decay length of the lateral mode,
        \((L/\mu)\sqrt{\Dy/\Dx}\) (Sec.~\ref{sec:mode}).
  \item[A4.] Lateral quasi-steady state: the profile across the section
        adjusts on the time scale \(L^{2}/\Dx\), which is short compared with
        the time scales on which \(\Cb\) and \(L\) change.
  \item[A5.] Locally, the profile across the section is the fundamental
        separable mode of the steady diffusion equation
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:mode}). Higher modes decay over a shorter distance
        along the boundary and are neglected (Sec.~\ref{sec:validity}).
\end{enumerate}
No assumption is made on whether the interface is reaction- or
diffusion-limited, and \(C_{\Ox}\) is not assumed uniform across the section.

\subsection{Cross-section integration of the two-dimensional balance}
\label{sec:integration}

Under A1 the two-dimensional conservation equation in the oxide is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial t}
 =\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\!\left(\Dy\,\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial y}\right)
 +\Dx\,\frac{\partial^2 C_{\Ox}}{\partial x^2}.
\label{eq:2d}
\end{equation}
Each term is integrated over \(0\le x\le L(y,t)\), with the Leibniz rule
where the limit depends on \(y\) or \(t\).

\paragraph{Lateral term.} With A2 and the definition \eqref{eq:Jr-def},
\begin{equation}
\int_{0}^{L}\Dx\,\frac{\partial^2 C_{\Ox}}{\partial x^2}\,\dd x
 =\Dx\,\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial x}\bigg|_{0}^{L}
 =-\Jr .
\label{eq:int-lateral}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Time term.} Because the upper limit moves,
\begin{equation}
\int_{0}^{L}\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial t}\,\dd x
 =\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bigl(L\,\Cb\bigr)-\Ci\,\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}.
\label{eq:int-time}
\end{equation}
The last term is the dissolved oxygen, at concentration \(\Ci\), contained in
the oxide formed during \(\dd t\).

\paragraph{Along-boundary term.} Applying the Leibniz rule twice,
\begin{align}
\int_{0}^{L}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\!\left(\Dy\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial y}\right)\dd x
 &=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\int_{0}^{L}\Dy\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial y}\,\dd x
   -\Dy\,\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial y}\bigg|_{x=L}\frac{\partial L}{\partial y},
\\
\int_{0}^{L}\Dy\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial y}\,\dd x
 &=\Dy\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\bigl(L\,\Cb\bigr)-\Ci\,\frac{\partial L}{\partial y}\right]
 \notag\\
 &=\Dy\,L\,\frac{\partial\Cb}{\partial y}
   +\Dy\,(\Cb-\Ci)\,\frac{\partial L}{\partial y}.
\end{align}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\int_{0}^{L}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\!\left(\Dy\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial y}\right)\dd x
 ={}&\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\!\left(\Dy\,L\,\frac{\partial\Cb}{\partial y}\right)\\
  &+\underbrace{\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\!\left[\Dy(\Cb-\Ci)\frac{\partial L}{\partial y}\right]
   -\Dy\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial y}\bigg|_{x=L}\frac{\partial L}{\partial y}}_{O(\partial L/\partial y)} .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:int-y}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Result.} Collecting \eqref{eq:int-lateral}--\eqref{eq:int-y},
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bigl(L\,\Cb\bigr)
 =\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\!\left(\Dy\,L\,\frac{\partial\Cb}{\partial y}\right)
  -\Jr
  +\Ci\,\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}
  +O\!\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial y}\right),
\label{eq:1d-exact}
\end{equation}
which is exact for Eq.~\eqref{eq:2d}. Under A3 the last group is dropped.

At the moving interface the diffusive flux \(\Jr\) of \eqref{eq:Jr-def}
supplies both the reactions and the dissolved oxygen in the newly formed oxide
(Stefan condition, dissolved oxygen in the metal neglected):
\begin{equation}
\Jr=\sum_k\Jr^{k}(\Ci)+\Ci\,\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}.
\label{eq:stefan}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:stefan} into \eqref{eq:1d-exact}, the terms
\(\Ci\,\partial L/\partial t\) cancel and the sink is the reaction consumption
alone,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bigl(L\,\Cb\bigr)
 =\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\!\left(\Dy\,L\,\frac{\partial\Cb}{\partial y}\right)
  -\sum_k\Jr^{k},
\label{eq:1d-working}
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~\eqref{M-eq:O-mass} of the main text with the flux of
Eq.~\eqref{M-eq:O-flux}. The ratio of the Stefan term in \eqref{eq:stefan} to
the reaction term is the ratio of the dissolved oxygen concentration \(\Ci\)
to the concentration of the oxygen bound in the oxide, which is much smaller
than unity. The diffusive flux \(\Jr\) and the reaction consumption
\(\sum_k\Jr^{k}\) therefore differ by a negligible amount, and the single
symbol \(\Jr\) is used for both, as in the main text; in particular the
closure of Sec.~\ref{sec:mu} equates \(\Jr\) of \eqref{eq:Jr-def} to
\(\sum_k\Jr^{k}\).

\subsection{Lateral problem: separation of variables}
\label{sec:mode}

\subsubsection{Local problem}

Under A3 and A4, in the neighbourhood of a given position along the boundary
the oxide occupies a half-strip \(0\le x\le L\) with \(L\), \(\Dx\), \(\Dy\)
and the reaction law treated as constants, and the concentration satisfies
the steady form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:2d},
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\Dx\,\frac{\partial^2 C_{\Ox}}{\partial x^2}
+\Dy\,\frac{\partial^2 C_{\Ox}}{\partial y^2}=0,\\
\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial x}\bigg|_{x=0}=0,
\qquad
-\Dx\,\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial x}\bigg|_{x=L}=\Jr\bigl(C_{\Ox}(L,y)\bigr).
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Separable solutions}

With \(C_{\Ox}=X(x)\,Y(y)\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:laplace} separates into
\begin{equation}
\frac{X''}{X}=-\frac{\Dy}{\Dx}\,\frac{Y''}{Y}=-\left(\frac{\mu}{L}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
where the separation constant is written as \(-(\mu/L)^2\) with \(\mu>0\),
because the \(x\)-problem must be oscillatory (it has a homogeneous Neumann
condition at \(x=0\) and a sink at \(x=L\)) and the \(y\)-dependence must
decay into the boundary. Then
\begin{equation}
X=\cos\!\left(\frac{\mu x}{L}\right),
\qquad
Y=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\mu y}{L}\sqrt{\frac{\Dx}{\Dy}}\right),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
C_{\Ox}(x,y)=A\,\cos\!\left(\frac{\mu x}{L}\right)
 \exp\!\left(-\frac{\mu y}{L}\sqrt{\frac{\Dx}{\Dy}}\right),
\label{eq:mode}
\end{equation}
where \(A\) is an amplitude and \(\mu\) is to be fixed by the interfacial
condition. The dependence on \(x\) involves \(\Dx\) alone; the ratio of the
two diffusivities enters only the decay length along the boundary,
\((L/\mu)\sqrt{\Dy/\Dx}\). Different values of \(\mu\) give different modes;
the fundamental mode has the smallest \(\mu\) and the slowest decay in \(y\),
and by A5 it is the one retained.

\subsubsection{Averages and flux of the mode}

From \eqref{eq:mode}, with the factor depending on \(y\) absorbed into \(A\),
\begin{align}
\Cb&=\frac{A}{L}\int_{0}^{L}\cos\!\left(\frac{\mu x}{L}\right)\dd x
    =A\,\frac{\sin\mu}{\mu},
\label{eq:mode-avg}\\
\Ci&=A\cos\mu,
\label{eq:mode-int}\\
\Jr&=-\Dx\,\frac{\partial C_{\Ox}}{\partial x}\bigg|_{x=L}
    =\frac{\Dx\,A\,\mu\sin\mu}{L}.
\label{eq:mode-flux}
\end{align}
Eliminating the amplitude with \eqref{eq:mode-avg}, \(A=\mu\Cb/\sin\mu\),
gives the two relations of Eq.~\eqref{M-eq:film-transport} of the main text,
\(\Jr=\Dx\mu^{2}\Cb/L\) and \(\Ci=\Cb\,\mu\cot\mu\). Since \(\mu\cot\mu\)
decreases monotonically from \(1\) at \(\mu=0\) to \(0\) at \(\mu=\pi/2\),
\(0\le\Ci\le\Cb\) for \(\mu\in[0,\pi/2]\).

\subsubsection{Consistency with the averaged equation}

Substituting the mode into the steady form of \eqref{eq:1d-working} with
constant \(L\), \(\Dx\) and \(\Dy\),
\begin{equation}
\Dy L\,\frac{\partial^2\Cb}{\partial y^2}
 =\Dy L\,\frac{\Dx}{\Dy}\left(\frac{\mu}{L}\right)^2\Cb
 =\frac{\Dx\,\mu^2\,\Cb}{L}
 =\Jr ,
\end{equation}
with no residual. The averaged equation, which carries \(\Dy\), and the mode
closure, which carries \(\Dx\), are therefore mutually consistent; no
additional approximation is introduced by combining them.

\subsection{The equation for \(\mu\)}
\label{sec:mu}

\subsubsection{General reaction law}

Imposing the interfacial condition of \eqref{eq:laplace} on the mode gives
Eq.~\eqref{M-eq:qss-closure} of the main text, written here as the root of
\begin{equation}
g(\mu)\equiv\frac{\Dx\,\mu^{2}\,\Cb}{L}-\Jr\bigl(\Cb\,\mu\cot\mu\bigr)=0 .
\label{eq:g}
\end{equation}
It is a single scalar equation in \(\mu\); the inputs are the local values of
\(\Cb\), \(L\), \(\Dx\) and the metal concentrations that enter
\(\Jr(\cdot)\). The diffusivity \(\Dy\) does not enter.

\paragraph{Existence and uniqueness.} On \((0,\pi/2)\) the first term
increases monotonically from \(0\); the second term decreases monotonically,
because \(\Jr(\cdot)\) is nondecreasing and \(\mu\cot\mu\) is decreasing.
Hence \(g\) is strictly increasing, with
\begin{equation}
g(0^+)=-\Jr(\Cb),
\qquad
g(\pi/2)=\frac{\pi^2\Dx\,\Cb}{4L}-\Jr(0).
\end{equation}
If \(\Jr(\Cb)>0\) (oxidation possible at the local average concentration) and
\(\Jr(0)\le0\) (no consumption at zero oxygen, which holds for all three rate
laws), there is exactly one root \(\mu\in(0,\pi/2)\). If \(\Jr(\Cb)\le0\), no
oxygen is consumed: \(\mu=0\), \(\Jr=0\) and \(\Ci=\Cb\).

\paragraph{Newton iteration.} The derivative is
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
g'(\mu)=\frac{2\Dx\,\mu\,\Cb}{L}
        -\Jr'\bigl(\Ci\bigr)\,\Cb\,\frac{\dd}{\dd\mu}\bigl(\mu\cot\mu\bigr),\\
\frac{\dd}{\dd\mu}\bigl(\mu\cot\mu\bigr)=\frac{\sin\mu\cos\mu-\mu}{\sin^2\mu}<0,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
so \(g'>0\) throughout. A safeguarded Newton iteration is used, which falls
back to bisection if an iterate leaves \((0,\pi/2)\), started from the
linearized value of Sec.~\ref{sec:linear}.

\paragraph{Procedure at each grid point and time step.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Inputs: \(\Cb\), \(L\), \(\Dx\), \(C_{\mathrm{Cr}}^{\GB}\),
        \(C_{\mathrm{Fe}}^{\GB}\), \(C_{\mathrm{Si}}^{\GB}\).
  \item If \(\Jr(\Cb)\le0\): \(\mu=0\), \(\Jr=0\), \(\Ci=\Cb\).
  \item Otherwise \(g(\mu)=0\) is solved on \((0,\pi/2)\).
  \item \(\Jr=\Dx\mu^2\Cb/L\) and \(\Ci=\Cb\,\mu\cot\mu\); the individual
        \(\Jr^{k}(\Ci)\) give the metal sinks,
        Eqs.~\eqref{M-eq:sink-cr}--\eqref{M-eq:sink-si} of the main text, and
        the thickness rates,
        Eqs.~\eqref{M-eq:thick-cr2o3}--\eqref{M-eq:thick-sio2}.
  \item \(\Jr\) is the sink in Eq.~\eqref{M-eq:O-mass} of the main text.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Linear reaction law}
\label{sec:linear}

For \(\Jr=k\,\Ci\), with \(k=\dd\Jr/\dd\Ci\) as in the main text,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:g} becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Dx\mu^2}{L}=k\,\mu\cot\mu
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\mu\tan\mu=\frac{kL}{\Dx}\equiv\mathrm{Da},
\label{eq:mu-linear}
\end{equation}
the classical eigenvalue condition for a Robin boundary. \(\mathrm{Da}\) is
the ratio of the diffusion resistance across the section, \(L/\Dx\), to the
reaction resistance \(1/k\). The concentration \(\Cb\) drops out: \(\mu\)
depends only on \(\mathrm{Da}\) (Table~\ref{tab:mu}).

In the reaction-limited limit, \(\mathrm{Da}\ll1\), the profile across the
section is nearly uniform, with a parabolic deviation of relative size
\(O(\mathrm{Da})\), and the decay length along the boundary is
\((L/\mu)\sqrt{\Dy/\Dx}\simeq\sqrt{\Dy L/k}\), much larger than \(L\). In the
diffusion-limited limit, \(\mathrm{Da}\gg1\), the profile across the section
is a quarter cosine and the decay length along the boundary is
\((2L/\pi)\sqrt{\Dy/\Dx}\). Between these limits \(\mu\) interpolates
smoothly.

\subsection{Range of validity}
\label{sec:validity}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Higher modes.} The interfacial condition admits a sequence of
        eigenvalues \(\mu_0<\mu_1<\dots\), with
        \(\mu_n\in(n\pi,\,n\pi+\pi/2)\) for a Robin condition. Each mode
        decays along the boundary over the distance
        \((L/\mu_n)\sqrt{\Dy/\Dx}\). The first higher mode therefore decays
        over at most \((L/\pi)\sqrt{\Dy/\Dx}\), compared with at least
        \((2L/\pi)\sqrt{\Dy/\Dx}\) for the fundamental. Higher modes are
        excited where the boundary data change abruptly: the surface entry,
        the oxidation tip, and any position where \(L\), the diffusivities or
        the metal concentrations jump. Away from such places only the
        fundamental survives (A5).
  \item \textbf{Slowly varying geometry (A3).} The mode is derived for a
        strip of constant width. For a wedge-shaped oxide the closure is
        applied with the local \(L(y)\); the error is
        \(O(\partial L/\partial y)\), the same order as the terms dropped in
        \eqref{eq:1d-exact}.
  \item \textbf{Quasi-steady lateral profile (A4).} \(L^2/\Dx\) must be short
        compared with the time scales of \(\Cb(t)\) and \(L(t)\).
  \item \textbf{Early stage.} When the depth of the oxidized zone is
        comparable to the decay length \((L/\mu)\sqrt{\Dy/\Dx}\), the whole
        problem is two-dimensional. The one-dimensional description is
        meaningful only once the oxidized zone is slender; the initial stage
        lies outside its scope.
  \item \textbf{Equivalent homogeneous medium (A1).} The harmonic mean
        \(\Dx\) is exact for a one-dimensional steady flux across the
        section; for a nonuniform \(D_{\Ox}(x)\) the fundamental mode is not
        a pure cosine, and the deviation is neglected. The arithmetic mean
        \(\Dy\) is exact when the gradient along the boundary is uniform
        across the section; the correlation between \(D_{\Ox}(x)\) and the
        variation of \(\partial C_{\Ox}/\partial y\) across the section is
        neglected.
\end{enumerate}
