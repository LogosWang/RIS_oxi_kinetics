# Model survey C: mesoscale intergranular corrosion / oxidation models (phase-field, cellular automata, peridynamics, FEM, analytical)

Purpose: for each paper, establish whether the model (a) uses a constant grain-boundary (GB) transport coefficient and yields an analytical/empirical penetration law, (b) makes transport depend on the type/composition of the corrosion product or oxide already formed (or on porosity), and (c) takes alloy/GB composition as input and predicts an evolving product composition. This is to position the manuscript's model: a 1D oxygen transport equation along a GB of 304 SS in PWR primary water with an effective diffusivity D_eff that depends on the local oxide phase mixture (Cr2O3, Fe3O4, FeCr2O4, SiO2 with SiO2 dissolution leaving porosity), interfacial reaction rates that depend on RIS-modified GB concentrations of Cr, Fe and Si, and oxide-thickness growth, giving intergranular oxide penetration depth vs time and dose.

Reading method: every PDF was converted to text page by page (PyMuPDF) and read completely, skipping only reference lists. Page numbers below are the printed journal page numbers where the PDF shows them; "PDF p." denotes the page index in the PDF file. For the thesis, pages are the thesis-printed page numbers with the PDF index in brackets. All quotations are verbatim from the extracted text (ligatures normalised). Where the text extraction of an equation was ambiguous this is flagged.

None of the ten models makes the GB transport coefficient a function of the local oxide phase assemblage or of porosity left by dissolution of a product, and none couples the GB composition to a dose-dependent process. Only the two de Rancourt works (1, 10) treat an oxide phase with transport through it, and in both the oxide is a single stoichiometric Cr2O3 with constant diffusivities.

## 1. derancourt2015modelling

**Citation (as printed):** Victor de Rancourt, *Modelling the oxidation of polycrystalline austenitic stainless steels using a phase field approach coupled with mechanics* (French title: *Modelisation de l'oxydation des aciers inoxydables polycristallins par une approche en champs de phases couplee avec la mecanique*), These de doctorat, Ecole Nationale Superieure des Mines de Paris (Mines ParisTech, Centre des Materiaux), Specialite "Sciences et Genie des materiaux", defended 12 June 2015; supervisors S. Forest and E. P. Busso, co-supervision B. Appolaire; HAL tel-01253941, NNT 2015ENMP0021 (PDF p. 1-2).

**Chapters read in full:** Table of contents (PDF p. 11-14); Chapter 1 Introduction (thesis pp. 2-9, PDF 20-27); Section 2.2 "Oxide growth mechanisms" including 2.2.4 "Grain boundary oxidation" and 2.2.5 (thesis pp. 38-47, PDF 56-65); Chapter 3 "Formulation of phase field models in the framework of continuum thermodynamics" (thesis pp. 58-103, PDF 76-121); Chapter 4 "Application to oxidation of austenitic stainless steels single crystals under mechanical load" (thesis pp. 110-143, PDF 128-161); Chapter 5 "Grain boundary oxidation" (thesis pp. 146-178, PDF 164-196); Chapter 6 "Conclusions" (thesis pp. 184-188, PDF 202-206); Appendix A "Calculation of growth rate constants" (thesis pp. 192-201, PDF 210-219). Not read: Section 2.1 (materials review, thesis pp. 15-37), Appendices B-H (Jacobian, interface calibration, elastic analyses, scaling, mesh, scripts), and the reference lists.

**Model type and governing equations (plain words):**
- Two-phase phase field phi (phi = 0 in austenite gamma-Fe, 1 in Cr2O3) with multicomponent diffusion (O, Cr, and Ni in the quaternary case) and mechanics, solved by the finite element method (code Zebulon/Zset) in a monolithic Newton scheme (Section 3.7, thesis p. 77).
- Free energy = interface part (double well + gradient, Eq. 3.15-3.16) + chemical part (quadratic potentials per phase, Eq. 3.32) + mechanical part (Eq. 3.33-3.34), each phase potential interpolated with h(phi) = phi^2(3-2phi) (Eq. 3.1-3.3, 3.17).
- Mass balance Eq. 3.5; mechanical equilibrium Eq. 3.6; phase-field "generalised stress" balance Eq. 3.8.
- Fick-type fluxes derived from a dissipation potential: J_i = -L_i grad(mu_i) with mobility L related to diffusivity by Eq. 3.43, and effective mobility in the diffuse interface L_i(phi) = h(phi) L_i^alpha + h-bar(phi) L_i^beta (Eq. 3.44-3.45).
- Allen-Cahn equation Eq. 3.50-3.52 (Helmholtz/Reuss) and Eq. 3.78-3.80 (Gibbs/Voigt); the Voigt/Taylor scheme (equal strains, Eq. 3.67) is retained for all oxidation simulations.
- Viscoplastic power law in the oxide (Eq. 3.46, 4.21), crystal plasticity in the metal (Eq. 3.47-3.48, 4.22-4.24).
- Chapter 4 (planar oxide, 1D): operative tie-line system Eq. 4.3; parabolic rate constant Eq. 4.4 and Appendix A (K_p = 2 D_O^alpha Omega_O, Eq. A.8-A.10; supply-of-alloying-element law Eq. A.36; Wagner law Eq. A.72); Cr-depleted-zone law Eq. 4.13-4.17.
- Chapter 5 (bicrystal, 2D): Fisher GB model and its analytic solution Eq. 5.1; diffuse GB field eta(y) = 1 - tanh^2((y - y_GB)/(lambda/2)) (Eq. 5.3) and orientation field theta (Eq. 5.4); anisotropic GB diffusivity matrix (Eq. 5.2, with D_y = D_bulk, D_x = D_z = D_GB, thesis p. 150); GB excess energy f_GB = h-bar(phi) chi eta (Eq. 5.6) entering the driving force (Eq. 5.8) and Young-Laplace wetting (Eq. 5.9-5.11); GB/bulk diffusivity ratio R_GB (Eq. 5.15); damage model Eq. 5.20-5.25 with damage-dependent diffusivity Eq. 5.26.

**Transport / oxidation kinetics:** Diffusivities are constants per phase and per region (bulk vs GB), taken from literature tables (Tables 4.1-4.3, thesis pp. 116-118) and re-assessed against Sabioni's rate constants (Section 4.1.4). GB transport is a Fisher-type short circuit with a fixed enhancement ratio: "enhanced grain boundary diffusion is here treated by modifying the ratio between bulk and grain boundary diffusion, denoted R_GB. Then, such a ratio is the same in the oxide and in the substrate for simplicity ... During the following simulations, this ratio will be set to 10, 100, and 1000 as beyond such values, the oxide intrusion becomes needle shaped." (thesis p. 164, PDF 182). The oxide-side oxygen boundary condition drives growth: "The prescribed concentration of O2- at the free surface governs the growth kinetics." (p. 115). Because the oxide is stoichiometric, "The equilibrium concentration of oxygen at the metal oxide interface is almost constant so that the diffusivity of oxygen within the oxide is the dominant parameter in the selection of the metal oxide interface morphology." (p. 165). The oxygen diffusivity in Cr2O3 is fixed at D_O = 6 x 10^-18 m^2/s (p. 122) after the re-assessment "0.01 < D_O^Cr2O3 [um^2/h] < 0.45" from Sabioni's k_p via K_p = 2 D Omega_O (Eq. 4.5-4.6). Transport does not depend on oxide composition (one oxide only), nor on porosity: the assumptions list states that the following are neglected, "the nucleation of the oxide layer, the influence of charges (electrochemistry), the Kirkendall-effect or vacancy fluxes (evolution of porosities), the brittle failure of the oxide layer, the oxide heterogeneous grain size distribution and the external oxide layer (Duplex oxide)." (p. 112, PDF 130). A composition-dependent diffusivity is explicitly flagged as missing: "One can note that the depletion levels can be large and that a concentration-dependent diffusivity can significantly modify the current results." (p. 168).

**Alloy/GB composition as input; evolving product composition:** The substrate composition is an input: "the substrate initially contains 16% of chromium and 10% of nickel, which are equally imposed by Dirichlet boundary conditions far away from the interface in the substrate." (p. 118). The model predicts Cr depletion and Ni enrichment at the interface ("the present simulations are shown to reproduce the local nickel enrichment and the chromium depletion discussed in Chapter 2", p. 119) and a parabolic growth of the Cr-depleted zone (K_Cr = 0.14 um^2/h, Fig. 4.12, Eq. 4.17). However, composition does not feed back on kinetics: "the chromium concentration (initial chromium content and chromium depletion) has been shown here to have no role on the growth kinetics" (p. 127). Around an intrusion, "A depleted chromium area is observed at the shoulders of the oxide intrusion, whereas a local enrichment of chromium is observed at the tip of the oxide intrusion. Away from the oxide intrusion and within the grain boundary, the chromium concentration field is depleted as observed in the literature" (p. 168). The oxide composition does not evolve: the oxide is "a stoichiometric oxide layer" (p. 112) and its free-energy curvature is "chosen to be sufficiently high to prevent the chemical composition of the oxide to deviate from stoichiometry" (p. 113). No Fe-bearing spinel, magnetite or SiO2 is considered.

**Irradiation / RIS:** Not modelled. Mentioned only as context/perspective: "The thesis should then provide a framework for the investigation of numerous damage mechanisms in a near future, such as irradiation damage for instance." (p. 8) and "stress corrosion cracks in austenitic stainless steels are frequently found to grow along the grain boundary network, which is particularly true in the case of irradiation assisted stress corrosion cracking." (p. 148). The thesis is also not a PWR-water model: "Despite a large amount of experimental data available at elevated temperatures in PWRs, the thesis has been mainly focused on high temperature oxidation for simplicity." (p. 4).

**What is predicted and against what data:**
- Planar Cr2O3 growth at 900 C is parabolic, "K ~ 0.02 um^2.h^-1" (Eq. 4.4, Fig. 4.5, p. 120); compared in Fig. 4.14(a) with "Sabioni, 2012" and used to re-assess D_O from Sabioni et al. (2012) k_p values for 304 SS (Table 4.6, p. 122: k_p = 0.151 um^2/h at 900 C).
- Cr-depleted zone width grows as x_med = sqrt(0.15 t) (Eq. 4.17, p. 127), checked against the semi-analytic erf solution.
- Grain boundary diffusion: the diffuse anisotropic GB reproduces Fisher's analytic solution (Fig. 5.4-5.5, pp. 151-154); the isotropic diffuse GB "is shown to be unable to deeply penetrate the base metal" (p. 152).
- Intrusion kinetics: "The resulting time exponents, which are obtained by means of linear regressions, are 0.45 for the bulk and 0.31 for the tip of the oxide intrusion, which is close to what [Irving, 1964] proposed." (p. 166, Fig. 5.14); elsewhere the intrusion is said to slow "because this phenomenon is in t^1/4" (p. 175). Wetting has "a negligible impact on the metal-oxide interface morphology" (p. 168).
- Stress fields: ~400 MPa tensile hydrostatic stress at the intrusion tip (p. 171); GB opening stress at the free surface becomes tensile with time (p. 175); a 200 MPa creep load accelerates this (Fig. 5.25).
- Experimental comparison of the intrusion depth vs time is only qualitative (Lozano-Perez et al. 2012 bimodal oxide depth distribution, Fig. 5.13, p. 166); no intrusion-depth data set is fitted.

**Authors' stated limitations:** Assumption list on p. 112 (quoted above). "It is worth recalling that the short circuit diffusion behaviour of the oxidised grain boundary (inside the oxide layer) is to date not fully understood. Nevertheless, preliminary simulations showed that it is needed for the formation of deep intergranular oxide intrusions." (p. 164). "First, the chemical free energies were simplified by quadratic potentials. The model needs first to be enhanced with logarithmic chemical free energies provided by accurate thermodynamic data ... A comprehensive description of the diffusion short circuit behaviour of oxidised grain boundaries has still to be done." (p. 188). "The results on damage are unfortunately not presented in the work" (p. 178). Chapter 4 is "restricted to the oxidation of single crystals, which is why only unidimensional simulations are performed" (p. 103).

**Relation to the manuscript's model:** The thesis is the closest mechanistic ancestor (oxygen transport along a GB short circuit driving a Cr2O3 intrusion, with explicit Cr/Ni alloy composition and a Fisher-type GB diffusivity), but it uses a single stoichiometric oxide with constant, composition-independent diffusivities, finds no feedback of Cr content on kinetics, and contains neither multi-oxide D_eff, porosity, Si/SiO2, PWR water chemistry nor RIS/dose dependence.

## 2. ansari2020multiphasefield

**Citation (as printed):** Talha Qasim Ansari, Jing-Li Luo and San-Qiang Shi, "Multi-Phase-Field Model of Intergranular Corrosion Kinetics in Sensitized Metallic Materials", *Journal of The Electrochemical Society* 167 (2020) 061508, DOI 10.1149/1945-7111/ab856d (PDF p. 2 = article p. 1; article pages 1-10 = PDF 2-11).

**Model type and governing equations:** Multi-phase-field (MPF) electrochemical dissolution model solved by FEM (COMSOL, Galerkin + BDF, p. 5). Total Gibbs free energy = chemical + gradient + electrostatic (Eq. 2); chemical energy with a fourth-order Landau multi-well f0 and ideal-solution ionic terms (Eq. 3-4); gradient energy (Eq. 5); electrostatic energy with charge density (Eq. 6-7). Reaction rate from Butler-Volmer-type net rate (Eq. 8-9) with driving force = total overpotential (Eq. 10). Order-parameter evolution for each phase k (grain, sensitized GB, electrolyte) Eq. 11, with a saturation factor S = 1 - c_Al3+/c_sat, constraint sum eta_k = 1 (Eq. 12), overpotential psi_k = phi - (E_app - E_cor^k) (Eq. 13), interface localisation functions (Eq. 14-15). Ion transport by Nernst-Planck Eq. 16 with effective diffusivity D_i^eff = sum_j D_i^j H_j (Eq. 17); electrostatic potential by Poisson/Ohm Eq. 18 with current density from the reaction rate Eq. 19.

**Transport / corrosion kinetics:** Transport is only in the electrolyte, with a constant ion diffusivity (D_i^e = 5.53 x 10^-10 m^2/s, zero in metal; Table I, p. 4). "The diffusion coefficient varies from one phase to another. Therefore, it is expressed as a function of phase fractions" (p. 3). Kinetics are electrochemical: "Corrosion potential E_cor^k is a material property and varies with material composition and crystal orientation. Therefore, each grain, crystal orientation and SGB has different corrosion potential values." (p. 3). No analytical law: depth(t) is a simulation output and "the IGC depth has a non-linear relation suggesting that the process is transport controlled" (p. 5); "The MPF model predicts that the process will go through a transition from being reaction-controlled to transport-controlled after 50 h of electrochemical exposure." (p. 7). No corrosion product is present in this paper (the authors' earlier model with insoluble corrosion product is only cited, p. 2); no porosity.

**Alloy/GB composition as input; evolving product composition:** Composition enters indirectly through assigned corrosion potentials: beta-phase (Al3Mg2) at sensitized GBs with E_cor = -0.92 V_SCE, grain -0.75 V_SCE (pp. 5-6). "In Al-Mg alloys, Mg diffuses towards the SGBs ... to form beta-phase (Al3Mg2). This results in a local galvanic couple in which the element composition near the SGBs is significantly different than the grain matrix." (pp. 2-3). SGB width is a geometric parameter (w_sgb = 5 l, 2-10 um). No product composition is predicted.

**Irradiation / RIS:** Not mentioned.

**What is predicted and against what data:** IGC depth vs time for AA2024 foil in 0.1 M NaCl at -0.6 V_SCE compared with Bonzom and Oltra SEM data and with the Jafarzadeh peridynamic model (Fig. 5, p. 5: "PD model tends to overestimate the IGC rate at the start"); IGC depth vs time for sensitized AA5XXX (DoS 49 mg/cm^2) in 0.6 M NaCl at -0.73 V_SCE vs Lim et al. data and their linear statistical law "2.64 +/- 0.80 (nm s^-1)" (Fig. 9, p. 7); rolling-direction anisotropy (L vs S) qualitatively (Fig. 11, p. 8); 3D demonstration (Fig. 12). Mechanism: "The IGC mechanism resembles with crevice corrosion ... The slow movement of metal ions through thin electrolyte film ... makes the process migration controlled." (pp. 5-6).

**Authors' stated limitations:** "It should be noted that the grain orientation effect was not included in any of the examples reported in this work." (p. 4). "The difference lies in the non-uniform nature of corrosion in the experimental results which is uniform in the MPF simulations. This is due to the assumption of uniformly distributed SGB in the simulations." (p. 6). "the size of grain and SGB in the previous simulation was scaled up to reduce the computation cost" (p. 6). Surface energy "for the sake of simplicity, it is considered as constant" (p. 3).

**Relation to the manuscript's model:** Ansari treats aqueous dissolution of a sensitized GB with electrolyte transport and electrochemical kinetics but no solid oxide; it offers a template for phase-dependent effective diffusivity (Eq. 17) and for GB-specific kinetic parameters, yet has no oxide composition, porosity, Cr/Si chemistry or irradiation dependence.

## 3. dicaprio20163d

**Citation (as printed):** D. di Caprio, J. Stafiej, G. Luciano, L. Arurault, "3D cellular automata simulations of intra and intergranular corrosion", *Corrosion Science* 112 (2016) 438-450, DOI 10.1016/j.corsci.2016.07.028.

**Model type and governing equations:** 3D stochastic cellular automaton on a cubic lattice (512 x 512 x 4096 sites) with three site types (intergranular, intragranular, solution). Grain geometries: cube piling (CP), brick piling (BP), isotropic Voronoi tessellation (IVT) and anisotropically stretched Voronoi (rolled, L/LT/ST). Rules: a metal site adjacent to solution converts to solution with probability p_ig (intergranular, set to 1) or p_g (grain, < 1); disconnected material is removed (p. 440). Observables: mean height h-bar(t) (Eq. 1) and roughness (Eq. 2). Groove angle relation p_ig/p_g = 1/sin(alpha/2) (Eq. 3) and, with p_ig = 1, sin(alpha/2) = p_g (Eq. 4, p. 445).

**Transport / corrosion kinetics:** No transport equation; constant probabilities set the kinetics. "The intergranular material can corrode with a probability p_ig which can be set to p_ig = 1. Choosing a different value rescales the correspondence between the simulation time step and the real corrosion rate." (p. 439). Time scale: "a cube of lateral dimension a = 0.2 um is chosen for the pixel size ... The time scale is set considering a corrosion process for the intergranular material of 1 mm/y. With the above value of a, the simulation time step corresponds to 6310 s." (p. 439). Resulting laws are linear: "The linear regression lines for both models are quasi identical, the expression is h_lr(t) = 4141 - 0.9994 t" (p. 440); for IVT "h_lr(t) = 4151 - 0.8090 t. Here, the effective corrosion rate of the material is 0.809 mm/y." (p. 442). No corrosion product, no oxide, no porosity.

**Alloy/GB composition as input; evolving product composition:** Composition is discussed only as motivation: "A common occurrence is segregation or depletion of some components of the alloys in the intergranular region ... In this paper, the focus is on the difference in corrosion kinetics between intra and intergranular corrosion. Consequently, the dependence of corrosion on crystalline orientation is not taken into account." (p. 439). "The corrosion rates of inter and intragranular material depend on the treatment and also on aggressiveness (temperature, pressure, composition) of the external environment" (p. 439), but these are lumped into p_ig, p_g. No product composition.

**Irradiation / RIS:** Not mentioned.

**What is predicted and against what data:** Height (penetration) vs time (Fig. 1, 9), roughness oscillations tied to grain-layer detachment (Fig. 2, 10), cross-section morphologies and V-shaped grooves (Figs. 3-6, 14-20, 23), direction dependence for rolled microstructure: "the fastest and slowest corrosion rates - respectively the directions of corrosion parallel to (L) and (ST) axes - differ by a factor of approximately 1.8" (p. 442). Comparison with experiment is qualitative: time scale anchored on Gwinner et al. [17] ("a period of 1000 h corresponds to the detachment of one layer of grains of characteristic dimension 84 um ... approximately to 0.74 mm/y", p. 439); groove angles 5-15 deg of [17] mapped to p_g = 0.087 and ~25 deg of Bague et al. [57] to p_g = 0.22 (p. 446); SEM image of AE44 Mg alloy (Fig. 13) resembles simulated surface (p. 444).

**Authors' stated limitations:** "Clearly, the order of magnitude is only indicative, as real systems may have grains of different dimensions depending on the metallurgical treatment or the 'history of the sample'." (p. 439). "The modelling is very simple. The grain cores are embedded in intergranular material. Both the core and intergranular materials are homogeneous and isotropic." (p. 439).

**Relation to the manuscript's model:** A purely geometric/probabilistic IGC model with constant rates and a linear penetration law; it has no transport, oxide or composition physics and serves only as a contrast for grain-detachment-controlled kinetics in aggressive media.

## 4. guiso2020intergranular

**Citation (as printed):** Simone Guiso, Dung di Caprio, Jacques de Lamare, Benoit Gwinner, "Intergranular corrosion: Comparison between experiments and cellular automata", *Corrosion Science* 177 (2020) 108953, DOI 10.1016/j.corsci.2020.108953.

**Model type and governing equations:** 3D stochastic CA on a hexagonal close-packed grid (1280^3 cells), states IGN (grain boundary), GRN (grain), SOL (solution); Voronoi microstructure (N_seed = 10^4, mean grain 272 cells, Eq. 10). Asynchronous update; effective probabilities P'_grn = P_grn N_b/N_b,max, P'_ign = P_ign N_b/N_b,max (Eq. 6a, 6b), i.e. dissolution probability proportional to the number of solution neighbours (p. 4). Stationary grain dissolution velocity and chemical roughness (Eq. 7-8); anisotropy deviation epsilon(theta, phi) (Eq. 9). Scaling factors A = 0.309 um/cell and B ~ 1.0 h/iteration (p. 6-7). Equivalent thickness loss ETL_sim (Eq. 11) and corrosion rate CR_sim (Eq. 12). Experimental semi-empirical model (Gwinner et al. 2016) recalled: ETL_exp (Eq. 1), CR_exp (Eq. 2), V_corr = (2/3)(V*_gb/D)(V*_gb - V_s) t + V_s for t <= 1.5 D/V*_gb and V_corr = V*_gb afterwards (Eq. 3-4), V_gb = V*_gb/cos(beta) (Eq. 5), with "V_s = 92 um/y, V*_gb = 1287 um/y and beta = 35 deg" (p. 4).

**Transport / corrosion kinetics:** No transport; constant probabilities calibrated on measured velocities: "The IGN corrosion probability P_ign is set equal to 1. Then, P_grn is set in order to have P_grn/P_ign ratio equal to the experimental ratio v_grn/v_ign, which in this case corresponds to V_s/V_gb ~ 0.0581" (p. 5). "It should be noted that, as a grey box approach, the method cannot predict corrosion rates, but it uses coefficients provided experimentally." (p. 13). "In this model, a simplified effect of the strength of the solution is introduced by connecting the corrosion probability to the amount of solution surrounding a corroded cell. In the future, it will also be possible to account for diffusion and concentration effects of corrosive compounds in solution." (p. 13). No oxide/product, no porosity.

**Alloy/GB composition as input; evolving product composition:** Not an input. The paper notes "The specific kinetics of IGN corrosion is related to the crystallographic misorientation of adjacent grains and to the chemical composition of these regions. GB are schematically represented as planes" (p. 5) and that the study concerns "non-sensitized" low-carbon SS (AISI 310L, p. 2-3). No product composition.

**Irradiation / RIS:** Not mentioned.

**What is predicted and against what data:** Against Gwinner et al. 2016 immersion tests of AISI 310L in boiling HNO3 + V(V) (p. 3): ETL and CR vs time (Fig. 17) with two regimes, "At the beginning, the corrosion rate grows linearly with time while the equivalent thickness loss squarely evolves with it. Then, a transition regime is observed between 600 h and 1000 h. After t = 1000 h, the corrosion rate becomes constant and the weight loss increases linearly with time." (pp. 8-10); groove angle distributions (Table 4: experimental mean alpha' 11.6/11.5 deg vs CA 10.9/9.9 deg at 641/1602 h); relative corroded surface (Fig. 18); distribution of deepest IGN-SOL points (Fig. 20) and its min/mean/max vs time (Fig. 21: "The average and the maximum interface depths present a linear trend", p. 11); standard deviation linear then square-root-of-time (p. 12).

**Authors' stated limitations:** Quotes above (p. 13); also "Some crystal twinning appears on the experimental image, which are not modelled by CA." (p. 8) and "Due to the limited dimensions of the simulated systems, the grain detachment events have a large impact on the corrosion progression and explain the oscillations at t > 1000 h." (p. 12).

**Relation to the manuscript's model:** Provides a calibrated statistical description of groove-driven IGC and grain dropping with constant rates, useful as a contrast case; it contains no transport in a product layer, no oxide phases and no composition or dose dependence.

## 5. guiso2022intergranular

**Citation (as printed):** S. Guiso, N. Brijou-Mokrani, J. de Lamare, D. Di Caprio, B. Gwinner, V. Lorentz, F. Miserque, "Intergranular corrosion in evolving media: Experiment and modeling by cellular automata", *Corrosion Science* 205 (2022) 110457, DOI 10.1016/j.corsci.2022.110457.

**Model type and governing equations:** Same 3D HCP CA as Guiso 2020 ("The CA automata model used in this paper is identical to the one developed in a previous work ... All details are given in the reference [33]", p. 6). Equivalent thickness loss delta_e = delta_m/(rho S) x 10^4 (Eq. 1); transient time to steady state 3D/(2 V_ign) (Eq. 2); Beaunier relation V_ign/V_grn = 1/sin(alpha/2) (Eq. 3). Input rates (Table 4): "severe" V_grn = 273, V*_ign = 1656, V_ign = 1954 um/y, transient 565 h; "soft" 12, 56, 66 um/y, transient 16703 h; tortuosity k = 1.18 (p. 8). ## 6. lishchuk2011cellular

**Citation (as printed):** S.V. Lishchuk, R. Akid, K. Worden, J. Michalski, "A cellular automaton model for predicting intergranular corrosion", *Corrosion Science* 53 (2011) 2518-2526, DOI 10.1016/j.corsci.2011.04.027.

**Model type and governing equations:** 3D probabilistic CA (Moore neighbourhood) with site states E (empty), S (electrolyte), G (grain), B (grain boundary), P (corrosion product), L (surface layer). Three probabilities: P_grain (B adjacent to G and S corrodes), P_bound (B adjacent to S only), P_layer (surface layer). With diffusion, B -> P and P performs a random walk into S/E sites (pp. 2519-2520). Al dissolution/hydrolysis reactions listed as Eq. (1)-(5) (p. 2519) but not solved. Parameters fitted by differential evolution to experimental path length or maximum depth (Sections 4, 6).

**Transport / corrosion kinetics:** Constant probabilities; transport only as random walk of the generic product: "The value of the effective diffusion coefficient is controlled through the simulation time scale, which is the real time corresponding to one CA time step." (p. 2520). "We assume the rate of corrosion of the grains to be negligible compared to the corresponding rate for the grain boundaries, and the rate of corrosion of the grain boundaries to be dependent upon whether the boundary sites are adjacent to the grains, and if they are, to be different for each grain." (p. 2519). Penetration law: "The time dependence is manifestly different without and with the effect of diffusion, tending to linear at long time durations in the former case, while changing to t^1/2 dependence, which is characteristic to diffusion, in the latter case." (p. 2521, Fig. 3). No composition dependence of product or transport; no porosity.

**Alloy/GB composition as input; evolving product composition:** Not an input (AA2024-T3 composition only tabulated, Table 1). The product P is unspecified chemically. "The precise meaning of the concentration of P depends upon the parameters of the real system" (p. 2520).

**Irradiation / RIS:** Not mentioned.

**What is predicted and against what data:** Corrosion path length and maximum depth vs immersion time for AA2024-T3 in ASTM G110 solution (Table 2: 104 um at 8 h to 314 um at 144 h), using a 3D EBSD microstructure. Fitted values without diffusion: "P_grain = 1, P_bound = 1.22 x 10^-4, P_layer = 6.14 x 10^-3. One time step corresponds to 0.865 h." (p. 2524); with diffusion: "P_grain = 1, P_bound = 8.56 x 10^-4, P_layer = 0.248. One time step corresponds to 0.0411 h." (p. 2525). Cross-validation (train on depth, validate on path length) favours the diffusion model (Fig. 8).

**Authors' stated limitations:** "In the current problem, there are two adjustable parameters and six data points ... The model should also be tested on an independent data set; this is not possible here given the scarcity of data" (p. 2524). "To be completely confident about these conclusions though, one would ideally wish for more data." (p. 2525). "the model with diffusion trained on path length does not generalise adequately." (p. 2525).

**Relation to the manuscript's model:** The only CA here with an explicit (generic) product diffusion step, giving the t^1/2 law; but transport is composition-independent, the product is unspecified, and nothing relates to oxide phases, Cr/Si chemistry or irradiation.

## 7. osawa1985computer

**Citation (as printed):** Motoaki Osawa and Fukujiro Ikezu (Faculty of Engineering, Tokyo Denki University), "Computer Simulation of Intergranular Corrosion of Austenitic Stainless Steel by Means of Finite Element Methods" (Japanese title: 有限要素法によるオーステナイト・ステンレス鋼の粒界腐食シミュレーション), *Boshoku Gijutsu* (防食技術) 34, 331-338 (1985); received 22 December 1984. Text in Japanese with English abstract; quotations below are my translations with the original given.

**Model type and governing equations:** One-dimensional finite element solution of chromium diffusion normal to the GB during sensitisation (1 um domain in 25 elements, 2 nm elements near the GB), Eq. (1) dc/dt = D_Cr d2c/dx2, discretised as Eq. (2) ([C] + dt [K]){beta}_{m+1} = [C]{beta}_m + {F} (p. 332). Two regimes: (i) carbide-formation-rate limited, with carbon consumption K(C - C_inf) per unit GB area, carbon depletion Eq. (3) giving C = (C0 - C_inf) exp(-3Kt/r) + C_inf (r = grain radius), K = 45570 exp(-52970/RT) fitted so that Strauss-test minimum sensitisation times correspond to GB Cr = 12 % (p. 333, Fig. 2); (ii) Cr-diffusion limited, with GB Cr fixed by Cr23C6 equilibrium Eq. (4) using K_f, gamma_Cr and a_C expressions (p. 332). The corrosion rate is then obtained by mapping the computed Cr profile onto measured dissolution rates of 5-20 Cr-9 Ni steels in EPR (Fig. 3) and Strauss (Fig. 4) solutions (pp. 333-334).

**Transport / corrosion kinetics:** D_Cr is a constant Arrhenius value from Strawstrom and Hillert ("クロムの拡散係数はC. Strawstromらの値を用いた", p. 332). Corrosion kinetics are purely empirical functions of local Cr content: "有限要素法による計算では, まず粒界近傍でのクロム濃度分布を求め, それぞれのクロム量に対応した腐食速度で溶解が表面より内部に向って進行すると仮定している" ("In the FEM calculation, the Cr concentration distribution near the GB is first obtained, and dissolution is assumed to proceed from the surface inwards at the corrosion rate corresponding to each Cr content", p. 337). No oxide/product, no transport in a product layer, no porosity.

**Alloy/GB composition as input; evolving product composition:** Yes, composition is the central input: "初期条件としてクロム濃度, 炭素濃度, ニッケル濃度, 結晶粒径, 温度及び時間のきざみを設定した後" ("after setting Cr concentration, C concentration, Ni concentration, grain size, temperature and time step as initial conditions", p. 332). The evolving quantity is the GB Cr profile (Fig. 5: GB Cr falls to 11.7 % at 1.8 ks and 9.7 % at 3.6 ks at 923 K, then recovers to 10.1 % at 360 ks; depleted width with Cr < 16 % about 240 nm vs ~220 nm by Chung and Szklarska-Smialowska, p. 334). Carbide is assumed Cr23C6 of fixed composition ("炭化物の凝集, 組成変化はないものとし", p. 332). No oxide composition.

**Irradiation / RIS:** Not mentioned.

**What is predicted and against what data:** Time-temperature-sensitisation (TTS) diagrams computed from the Cr profile for EPR charge (Fig. 6) vs measured EPR reactivation ratio (Fig. 7, 8) for SUS 304; Strauss-test attack maps (Fig. 9: attack when computed rate > ~5 x 10^-7 g cm^-2 h^-1, through-thickness failure above ~3 x 10^-6, p. 335); continuous-cooling sensitisation (Figs. 10-14). Agreement within 823-1023 K; over-prediction above 1073 K attributed to intragranular carbides (abstract and p. 337).

**Authors' stated limitations:** Dissolution geometry: the computed attack narrows with depth whereas real attack widens, so "計算上の腐食速度は測定結果の値より小さくなる傾向がある" ("the calculated corrosion rate tends to be smaller than the measured value", p. 337, Fig. 15). Intragranular carbides: "本計算法を1,073K以上の温度範囲まで適用できるようにするには粒内に析出する炭化物量を考慮する必要があり, これらについては今後の課題である" ("to apply this method above 1073 K the amount of intragranular carbide must be considered; this is a future task", p. 337). Carbide dissolution neglected but "炭化物自体の腐食も考慮する必要があろう" when depletion is weak and carbide content high (p. 337).

**Relation to the manuscript's model:** This is the earliest paper here in which GB Cr depletion computed from a diffusion equation is converted into a GB attack rate through a composition-dependent rate law, which is analogous in spirit to the manuscript's RIS-concentration-dependent interfacial rates; however it concerns thermal sensitisation and active dissolution with no oxide, no product-dependent transport and no irradiation.

## 8. jafarzadeh2018peridynamic

**Citation (as printed):** Siavash Jafarzadeh, Ziguang Chen, and Florin Bobaru, "Peridynamic Modeling of Intergranular Corrosion Damage", *Journal of The Electrochemical Society* 165 (7) C362-C374 (2018), DOI 10.1149/2.0821807jes.

**Model type and governing equations:** Bond-based peridynamic (nonlocal) diffusion of metal concentration over a bi-material solid/liquid domain: dC/dt = integral of micro-fluxes over the horizon (Eq. [1]), micro-flux J = k (C(x') - C(x))/|x'-x|^2 (Eq. [2]); micro-diffusivities from the liquid diffusivity K_L and the solid "dissolution affinity" K_S with harmonic average at the interface (Eq. [3]); K_S(eta) = K0 x 10^(eta/beta_a) from the anodic Tafel slope (Eq. [4]); one-point Gaussian quadrature (Eq. [5]); forward Euler (Eq. [6]); bond-breaking and damage index (Eq. [7]-[8]); concentration-dependent damage d(C) linear between C_solid and C_sat (Eq. [9]); separate affinities for GB and grain with harmonic average for GB-grain bonds (Eq. From the moment at which the solution is saturated at the front, according to the diffusion-control model, diffusion in the electrolyte controls the penetration rate." (p. C368). "GB thickness in the model does not affect the results as long as it is smaller than grain dimensions by orders of magnitude." (p. C368). The salt film is not modelled explicitly: "The dissolution holds used in our PD model are artificial, but with the goal of effectively representing, at a coarse level, the complex chemo-physical aspects of diffusion-controlled corrosion." (p. C365). No oxide, no porosity.

**Alloy/GB composition as input; evolving product composition:** GB and grain are two phases distinguished by Tafel kinetics: "In most materials susceptible to IGC, material composition near GBs differs slightly from grain cores. "Finding Tafel plots from a bulk material with a composition exactly the same as the GB area or exactly the same as the grain matrix area for various alloys is challenging." (p. C367). **Irradiation / RIS:** Not mentioned.

C370); grain + GB dissolution at +500 mV_SCE with calibrated K_GM = 0.002 um^2/s (Fig. 13-14); potential series 400-600 mV (Fig. 15). Kinetics: diffusion-controlled, i.e. decelerating ("Transport-limited regime slows down the corrosion rate in time", p. C369).

**Authors' stated limitations:** "penetration rate is slightly faster in the simulation than what is empirically observed ... The actual microstructure has grains with non-smooth shapes" (p. C369). "we used only a single, but representative, microstructure" (p. C370). "one would benefit from considering the effect of pH alteration from hydrolysis, IR-drop on overpotential ... and using finer discretization to maintain the physical volume ratio of GBs to grains." (p. C372).

**Relation to the manuscript's model:** A continuum (nonlocal) IGC model with a GB-specific kinetic parameter and transport-limited penetration, conceptually similar to a 1D diffusion-reaction along the GB, but its transport medium is the electrolyte with constant diffusivity and no solid oxide; no composition evolution or dose dependence.

## 9. beaunier1980kinetical

**Citation (as printed):** L. Beaunier, M. Froment and C. Vignaud (C.N.R.S. Research Group No 4, Physique des Liquides et Electrochimie, Universite Pierre et Marie Curie), "A kinetical model for the electrochemical grooving of grain boundaries", *Electrochimica Acta* 25 (1980) 1239-1246 (received 8 January 1980).

**Model type and governing equations:** Analytical geometric-kinetic model of transpassive GB grooving (Fe-Ni-Cr and Ni-Si alloys in 2N H2SO4). Dissolution-rate vectors V_s (surface), V_j (GB penetration) and V_F (groove facets) with |V_j| > |V_s|; the GB plane bisects the groove so facet and surface rates are equal (p. 1243). Equilibrium of rate vectors gives a constant dihedral angle alpha and D'/d = 1/sin(alpha/2) (Eq. 1, p. 1243), where D' is the depth removed at the GB and d the general thickness dissolved. Faraday's law Q = I t (Eq. 3), m = MQ/(ZF), thickness d = M I_s t/(Z F rho) (Eq. 4) and D' = M I_j t/(Z F rho) (Eq. 5), hence I_j/I_s = 1/sin(alpha/2) (Eq. 6). Groove dimensions relative to the final surface: D = (1/sin(alpha/2) - 1) d (Eq. 8), W = 2(1/sin(alpha/2) - 1) tan(alpha/2) d (Eq. 8'). Empirical current difference Delta I = I_j - I_s = A + B I_s (Eq. 10); GB current I_j = I_j0 + I_s/sin(alpha_0/2) (Eq. 12 and 21-22; the extracted text of Eq. 12 is partly garbled, the form is inferred from Eq. 6 and the surrounding text). Kinetic interpretation: Arrhenius/Tafel current with active-site concentration (Eq. 13-16); limit angle related to the difference in active-site formation energy, sin(alpha_0/2) = exp(-Delta_j(E_f)/RT) (Eq. 19; sign as printed is ambiguous in extraction), and C_j/C_s = 1/sin(alpha/2) (Eq. 20).

**Transport / corrosion kinetics:** Purely activation-controlled dissolution; mass transport is deliberately excluded by the choice of electrolyte: "A two normal (2N) aqueous sulfuric acid solution has been chosen in order to limit the current density, which avoids firstly, a rapid evolution of the surface, and, in the second place, control of the kinetics by mass transport" (p. 1239). Rates are constant in time: "the dihedral angle alpha of the grooves reaches a stable value after a certain time ... when this equilibrium limit value is attained, it is conserved even during long periods of attacks." (p. 1240). Depth is linear in dissolved thickness, i.e. in charge and time at constant current (Eq. 8). No oxide, no product transport, no porosity.

**Alloy/GB composition as input; evolving product composition:** Composition and segregation affect the measured parameters alpha_0 and I_j0 but are not predicted: "The reactivity of each metal or alloy to intergranular corrosion is then indicated by the value of the limit angle alpha_0, an intrinsic parameter of grain boundary." (p. 1242). "We suppose that for pure material without segregation or precipitation, the grain boundary parameters M, Z, rho are identical to those of the matrix." (p. 1243). Deviations of Eq. (9) are attributed to segregation: "This is not possible unless there is a segregation or a precipitation, even of little importance, as we have been able to prove for specimens of nickel containing between 0 and 2 wt% of silicon" (p. 1244). No product composition.

**Irradiation / RIS:** Not mentioned.

**What is predicted and against what data:** Groove width W, depth D and angle alpha vs applied potential and current density for Ni-Si alloys (Figs. 4-7); alpha rises asymptotically to alpha_0 at high current density (p. 1242); measured vs calculated W and D "In the majority of the cases, the percentage of the error is low, which proves the validity of our model." (p. 1244). For Ni in 2N H2SO4 at 25 C, "alpha_0 = 104 deg, ie ... Delta_j(E_f) = 32 mJ m^-1 [as printed] ... I_j0 = 0.43 mA/cm^2 and that at 1300 mV vs sce, there are 49 per cent more active sites at the grain boundaries than at the surface of the nickel." (p. 1246).

**Authors' stated limitations:** "If the groove is narrow or if the dissolution is anisotropic, equation (6) can be false." (p. 1244). "the cause of the initial step of intergranular corrosion is still unknown ... Our main purpose here is the next step, to propagation." (p. 1243).

**Relation to the manuscript's model:** Beaunier's model is the classic constant-rate, geometry-based penetration law (D proportional to time, groove angle fixed by V_j/V_s) that later CA papers (3-5) calibrate against; it has no transport, oxide or composition evolution and is the antithesis of a transport-limited, product-dependent oxidation model.

## Cross-paper assessment for the manuscript

1. Constant GB transport coefficient with analytical/empirical law: Beaunier 1980 (linear depth in charge/time, fixed groove angle), di Caprio 2016, Guiso 2020/2022 (constant probabilities; linear or two-regime laws), Lishchuk 2011 (linear without diffusion, t^1/2 with random-walk diffusion), Osawa 1985 (constant D_Cr, empirical rate vs Cr), Jafarzadeh 2018 (constant electrolyte diffusivity, transport-limited), Ansari 2020 (constant electrolyte diffusivity, numerical transition reaction -> transport control), de Rancourt 2015/2025 (constant per-phase diffusivities with fixed GB enhancement ratio; parabolic layer, t^0.31 to cubic intrusion kinetics).
2. Transport dependent on product/oxide composition or porosity: none. The nearest are de Rancourt 2015/2025, where D differs between metal and oxide phases and between bulk and GB, and 2025 where D_GB increases with mechanical damage (Eq. 37). No model includes multiple oxide phases, SiO2 dissolution or porosity-controlled D_eff.
3. Alloy/GB composition as input with evolving product composition: Osawa 1985 and the two de Rancourt works take alloy Cr (and C/Ni) as inputs and predict GB or near-interface Cr depletion; Ansari 2020 and Jafarzadeh 2018 encode composition only through corrosion potentials/Tafel data; none predicts an evolving oxide/product composition, and none includes irradiation or RIS.
