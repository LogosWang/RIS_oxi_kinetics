# Model survey A: grain-boundary oxidation kinetic models (full-text read)

Purpose: for each of the 11 papers below, establish whether the existing GB-oxidation kinetic model
(a) assumes a constant GB/oxide oxygen diffusivity and yields an analytical front-depth law,
(b) makes oxygen transport depend on the type/composition of the oxide already formed, and
(c) takes the alloy/GB composition (in particular an RIS-modified composition) as input and predicts an evolving oxide composition.

Method: every PDF was converted page-by-page to text (PyMuPDF; the Read tool could not rasterise PDFs on this machine because poppler is absent) and every page of every paper, including appendices, was read. Reference lists were skipped except where needed to identify a citation. Page numbers below are the printed journal page numbers where the PDF carries them; for the arXiv preprint (paper 1) they are the preprint page numbers (= PDF page numbers). Quotations are verbatim from the extracted text (ligatures and symbols normalised).

Summary table (details and quotations follow in the per-paper sections):

Conclusion for the manuscript: none of the eleven papers combines a composition-dependent, phase-dependent GB oxygen diffusivity with an RIS- (or otherwise) modified GB composition as input. Papers 1, 3, 5, 6 use a single constant D (or k_p); paper 9 uses phase-dependent constant D's but for a pure metal surface scale; papers 2 and 4 carry alloy composition into the oxide chemistry but keep oxygen transport either prescribed (2) or a constant anisotropic D (4). Irradiation appears only in paper 10, which is not an oxidation model.

## 1. xu2015modeling

**Citation as printed.** Zhijie Xu, Dongsheng Li, Daniel K. Schreiber, Kevin M. Rosso, and Stephen M. Bruemmer, "Modeling Selective Intergranular Oxidation of Binary Alloys" (PDF is arXiv preprint 1808.10397v1, Pacific Northwest National Laboratory; no journal name printed). The published version is J. Chem. Phys. 142, 014704 (2015), as cited in paper 2's reference 9 ("Z. J. Xu, D. S. Li, D. K. Schreiber, K. M. Rosso, and S. M. Bruemmer, J. Chem. Phys. 142, 014704 (2015)", Sushko et al. p. 214114-8).

**Model type and governing equations.** One-dimensional kinetics model for an oxidation front advancing along a GB in a binary alloy AB, adapted from Wagner's thick-film theory (p. 4). Oxygen diffuses in the oxide along the GB (Eq. 1, p. 5: ∂C_o/∂t = D_o ∇²C_o, no sink term); alloy elements A and B diffuse along the GB with a source/sink term R_i(C_i∞ − C_i) representing exchange with the adjacent grains (Eq. 2, p. 5). Oxidation occurs only at the front via A + (a/2)O2 → AO_a and B + (b/2)O2 → BO_b (Eqs. 3-4, p. 6) with first-order rate constants k_A, k_B and stoichiometric flux balances (Eqs. 5-6, p. 6); the oxide is a mixture of AO_a and BO_b with average molar density Eq. (7); front velocity from mass conservation Eq. (8) (p. 7). Dimensionless form Eqs. (9)-(13) (p. 8) with Damköhler numbers Da_A, Da_B, diffusivity ratios φ_i = D_o/D_i, mass-transfer rates r_i and oxide/alloy molar density ratios w_i. In a frame moving with the front, approximate exponential profiles (Eqs. 18-19, p. 10) give depletion length parameter β_i (Eqs. 21-22, p. 10) and three algebraic equations (23), (26), (27) (p. 11) for v_s, c_o^− and α (fraction of O flux consumed by A) at any given penetration depth L_s. Oxide mole fractions m_A, m_B, m_O from Eqs. (28)-(30) (p. 12). For a = b = 1, w_A = w_B the system reduces to Eqs. (31)-(33) (pp. 12-13); for a flat surface (r_i = 0) to Eqs. (34)-(40) (pp. 13-15), including the critical minor-element concentration c_B∞ ≥ 1/w (Eq. 36).

**Oxygen transport.** The GB oxygen diffusivity is a single constant effective parameter. p. 5: "D_o is the effective diffusion coefficient of oxygen along the grain boundary and D_i is the diffusion coefficient of element A or B." p. 6: "There is no oxygen consumption in the bulk region, and hence no oxygen consumption term in Eq. (1)." Its value is a literature estimate, p. 17: "The effective diffusivity of oxygen along a grain boundary was estimated to be 10^-6 cm^2/s [33]. This number is close to (but still less than) oxygen diffusion in water 10^-5 cm^2/s at 20 °C, and is many orders of magnitude larger than that for oxygen diffusion in bulk nickel oxide." It does not depend on oxide phase, oxide composition, porosity or alloy composition. The only place oxide identity enters transport is through the oxide molar densities (Eq. 7, p. 7; NiO vs Cr2O3 densities, p. 14) and the surface-vs-GB distinction, p. 15: "Da_A for surface oxidation can be much larger than that of intergranular oxidation because the diffusion of oxygen is much faster in a grain boundary than that in the lattice oxide".

**Alloy/GB composition input; evolving oxide composition.** Bulk alloy composition enters as the far-field boundary condition, with GB segregation explicitly neglected, p. 6: "C_i∞ is assumed to be the bulk element concentration without any significant grain boundary segregation, which is reasonable for the alloys under consideration." p. 18: "Boundary conditions for c_A and c_B are determined from the atomic fraction of each element in bulk alloys as c_A∞ = 0.95 and c_B∞ = 0.05." The oxide composition (mole fractions of A, B, O; Eqs. 28-30) is an output and varies with the mass-transfer rate r_A (p. 19: "The atomic compositions of Al and Cr in metal oxide also increase with r_A as shown in Fig. 15"), but it does not feed back into D_o.

**Irradiation / RIS.** Not mentioned.

**Predictions and comparisons.** (i) Flat-surface Ni-Cr: critical Cr fraction ≈ 22 % for chromia-dominated scale with w ≈ 4.55 (p. 15), compared with Croll & Wallwork data for Ni-8Cr and Ni-21.9Cr (pp. 15-16, Fig. 4). (ii) GB oxidation of Ni-5Cr and Ni-5Al at 360 °C: velocity vs depth, p. 18: "There is slight decrease of the penetration speed with increasing penetration depth because of the increasing length of transport for oxygen to the progressing oxidation front. With the first 100 µm of oxidation penetration, the velocity v_s is about 5×10^-8 mm/s for Ni-5Cr and 7×10^-8 mm/s for Ni-5Al respectively." (i.e. nearly linear, not parabolic, over 0-100 µm; Fig. 7 p. 29). Compared with SCC crack growth rates ~9×10^-8 (Ni-5Cr) and 2×10^-7 mm/s (Ni-5Al), p. 18. (iii) Depletion length ahead of the front, p. 19: "Quantitative calculation gives β_Cr = 1.2 and β_Al = 36.6, or the characteristic length of depletion of 0.8 µm (up to 9 µm observed in experiment) for Cr and 0.03 µm for Al (~100s of nm observed in experiment), respectively." (iv) p. 18: "the oxygen concentration within the oxide is almost constant and independent of the penetration depth for both alloys, due to the relatively fast penetration of oxygen along the grain boundary".

**Stated limitations.** p. 9: "This assumption hints an unchanged front shape that is not true at the very beginning." p. 17: "Obviously all these diffusivity estimates would benefit from more detailed experimental and molecular simulation study". p. 18: "Because our model presently neglects stress effects, this suggests a possible correlation between the SCC crack growth rate and the grain boundary oxidation penetration speed." p. 20: "In reality, however, grain boundary migration can complicate or obfuscate this simplified picture by modifying the effective mass flow of the minor element into the grain boundary."

**Relation to the manuscript.** This is the closest analytical antecedent (1D GB front, reaction at the front, element depletion ahead of it), but it uses one constant D_o and bulk composition without segregation, so the manuscript's phase-composition-dependent D_eff(Cr2O3/Fe3O4/FeCr2O4/SiO2-porosity) and RIS-modified GB concentrations are genuinely new relative to it.

## 2. sushko2015multiscale

**Citation as printed.** Maria L. Sushko, Vitaly Alexandrov, Daniel K. Schreiber, Kevin M. Rosso, and Stephen M. Bruemmer, "Multiscale model of metal alloy oxidation at grain boundaries", The Journal of Chemical Physics 142, 214114 (2015), doi 10.1063/1.4921940.

**Model type and governing equations.** Three-dimensional mesoscale Poisson-Nernst-Planck / classical DFT (PNP/cDFT) model informed by plane-wave DFT barriers. Steady-state Nernst-Planck fluxes for each mobile species (Eq. 2, p. 214114-3: −J_i = D_i(r)[∇ρ_i + ρ_i/(k_BT)(q_i e∇φ + ∇μ_id + ∇μ_ex)]), ∇·J_i = 0 (Eq. 3), Poisson equation (Eq. 4), excess free energy Eq. (5) with square-well short-range term Eqs. (6)-(7) whose depth equals DFT migration barriers in the alloy and oxide formation energies in the oxide (p. 214114-3). Appendix gives FMT hard-sphere and MSA electrostatic functionals. Geometry: oxide slab inside a 3.5 nm GB with a truncated front (Fig. 1).

**Oxygen transport.** No continuum GB oxygen diffusivity is used. Oxygen pathways are restricted to the oxide lattice and oxygen supply is assumed sufficient, p. 214114-2/-3: "a reduced model (Fig. 1(b)) was employed that limits oxygen diffusion pathways to those through the oxide phase and does not allow for oxygen diffusion in the alloy phase or across the oxide/alloy interface. The assumption was made that the oxygen concentration in the oxide region is sufficient for forming stoichiometric metal oxide with Cr2O3 structure for Ni-5Cr (5 at. % Ni in Ni matrix is substituted by Cr) and NiAl2O4 structure for Ni-4Al". Metal diffusivities are DFT constants, p. 214114-3: "Plane-wave DFT values for the diffusion coefficients of mobile species were used with D_Ni = 0.25 × 10^-26 cm^2/s, D_Cr = 1.00 × 10^-26 cm^2/s, and D_Al = 0.52 × 10^-26 cm^2/s." Oxide phase and porosity do affect oxygen access qualitatively, as an output: p. 214114-4: "the oxide has a nanoporous network structure, with connected pores running primarily along the grain boundary (Fig. 3). The average pore diameter was 0.9 nm, which provides channels for fast and continuous oxygen diffusion to the oxidation front." and p. 214114-5: "The combination of these two processes defines the effective Cr content at the oxidation front during steady state, which, in turn, defines the porosity of the oxide. Assuming fast oxygen diffusion through the pores in the oxide, the slower process of grain boundary Cr diffusion in the alloy defines the rate for Cr supply to the oxidation front and the overall rate of alloy oxidation." For Ni-4Al, p. 214114-6: NiAl2O4 has "1.5 nm wide pores in the direction parallel to the grain boundary and 0.6 nm pores in the perpendicular direction providing an additional pathway for oxygen diffusion through the oxide" (abstract, p. 214114-1).

**Alloy/GB composition input; oxide composition.** Alloy composition (Ni-5Cr, Ni-4Al) and GB character enter via DFT barriers, p. 214114-3: "activation barriers for elementary diffusion of Ni, Cr, and Al in Ni alloys were E_Ni = 1.08 eV, E_Cr = 0.81 eV, and E_Al = 0.70 eV for bulk alloys, and E_Ni = 0.35 eV, E_Cr = 0.09 eV, and E_Al = 0.15 eV for the higher energy Σ = 9/(221) grain boundary." The oxide phase is prescribed from experiment but element partitioning inside it is predicted; p. 214114-3: "mixed Cr2O3/NiO oxides were also considered to lift an unnecessary bias of the model toward specific oxide composition." p. 214114-4: "Only Cr and O are found in this region, while Ni enriches the grain boundary/oxide interface (Fig. 2), in agreement with experimental observations." Being steady-state, there is no time-evolving oxide composition and no penetration-depth law.

**Irradiation / RIS.** Not mentioned (only "materials degradation in nuclear systems", p. 214114-1).

**Predictions and comparisons.** Qualitative: Cr vacancy distribution tens of nm ahead of the front and Ni vacancies within 3 nm (p. 214114-5), compared qualitatively with APT Cr depletion of Schreiber et al. (ref. 22); porous Cr2O3 along the GB (p. 214114-4); void formation 4-8 nm ahead of the front in Ni-4Al linked to disconnected oxide clusters (p. 214114-6). No quantitative kinetics.

**Stated limitations.** p. 214114-4/-5: "Because the model was limited to describing the formation of continuous oxide, it does not yet encompass the possibility of more complex oxide distributions, such as formation of disconnected oxide clusters ahead of the oxidation front ... Extending the model to accommodate such alternative pathways and expanding the range of oxide phases considered simultaneously, e.g., Cr2O3, NiO, and NiCr2O4 for Ni-5Cr oxidation, will be a focus of our future work." p. 214114-6: "Although in the current model we did not consider oxygen diffusion through the alloy ... Extension of our model to include oxygen diffusion through the alloy phase ... is another focus of our future work."

**Relation to the manuscript.** Supports the manuscript's premise that oxide composition/porosity controls oxygen access and that Cr supply along the GB can be rate-limiting, but it is an atomistic steady-state picture with prescribed oxide phase and no penetration-depth law, so it does not provide a composition-dependent D_eff or dose dependence.

## 3. evans2013mechanism

**Citation as printed.** H.E. Evans, H.Y. Li and P. Bowen, "A mechanism for stress-aided grain boundary oxidation ahead of cracks", Scripta Materialia 69 (2013) 179-182.

**Model type and governing equations.** Short analytical communication. Unstressed intrusion length estimated from literature parabolic constants: Eq. (1) (p. 180) h_650 = 0.2×10^-6 exp[(258000/R)(1/873 − 1/923)]^0.5 (5/48)^0.5 for chromia; Eq. (2) Δh_NiO = (k_p t)^0.5 = (4×10^-16 × 5×3600)^0.5 = 2.7 µm for NiO. Stress effect via vacancy flux Eq. (3) (p. 181) J_v = −D_v[∂C_v/∂x − (C_v Ω_B/kT)∂σ/∂x], stress-modified boundary vacancy concentration Eq. (4) (C_v)_σ = (C_v)_o exp(σΩ/kT), Ω = Ω_M(Φ−1) from the Pilling-Bedworth ratio Eq. (5), Fick's-law flux along the intrusion Eqs. (6)-(8), and the SAGBO enhancement ratio Eq. (9) S_σ = exp[σ_it Ω_M(Φ−1)/kT].

**Oxygen transport.** Constant D_v; parabolic growth assumed; one oxide species per intrusion. p. 180: "The two results may be compared by assuming parabolic growth kinetics of the chromia layer (i.e. its thickness h = (k_p t)^0.5) and an Arrhenius activation energy on the rate constant, k_p, of 258,000 J mol^-1, typical for chromia growth". p. 181: "The actual diffusion paths are likely to be oxide grain boundaries and the diffusing defect is likely to be a grain-boundary analogue of an oxygen vacancy" and "where ℓ is the length of the intrusion which, for simplicity, is assumed to be composed of just one oxide species." The oxide type enters through k_p and Φ (NiO 1.65, Cr2O3 2.07; p. 180), not through a composition-dependent diffusivity. The abstract (p. 179) states: "A stress-aided oxidation rate parameter is defined that is stress dependent and varies with the nature of the oxide."

**Alloy/GB composition input; oxide composition.** Alloy composition is not an input; the oxide identity is taken from Kitaguchi et al.'s TEM (Table 1, p. 180: case B Cr2O3, case C NiO). No oxide composition evolution is predicted.

**Irradiation / RIS.** Not mentioned.

**Predictions and comparisons.** Enhancement ratios observed/expected: 17.6 (Cr2O3, 3.0 vs 0.17 µm) and 3.6 (NiO, 9.7 vs 2.7 µm) (Table 1, p. 180). Predicted S_σ, p. 182: "values of 1.8 for NiO and 3.1 for Cr2O3 are predicted at a normal stress of 1 GPa at the tip of the inclusion, whereas at 3.0 GPa, the corresponding values for S_σ are 5.4 and 21.6." Compared against intrusion lengths of Kitaguchi et al. [14].

**Stated limitations.** p. 180: "This provides an upper estimate since any diffusional blocking by the NiO intrusion existing ahead of the crack prior to the dwell period has been neglected." p. 180: "Detailed computations have yet to be performed but a schematic illustration ... is shown". p. 181: "Integration of this expression is not possible here because σ_it is also likely to be dependent on intrusion length, ℓ, but in a manner that is currently unknown." p. 181: "these various assumptions, although plausible, are really intended to assess the maximum effect of stress on local vacancy equilibria."

**Relation to the manuscript.** Provides the canonical constant-k_p parabolic baseline for GB oxide intrusions and a stress multiplier; it neither treats oxide-composition-dependent transport nor alloy composition, so it is a point of contrast for the manuscript's D_eff formulation.

## 4. fang2022numerical

**Citation as printed.** C.Z. Fang, H.C. Basoalto, M.J. Anderson, H.Y. Li, S.J. Williams, P. Bowen, "A numerical study on the influence of grain boundary oxides on dwell fatigue crack growth of a nickel-based superalloy", Journal of Materials Science & Technology 104 (2022) 224-235, doi 10.1016/j.jmst.2021.06.045.

**Model type and governing equations.** 2D ABAQUS finite-element submodel of a crack-tip GB with: stress-assisted oxygen flux j_O = −D_O·∇C_O − κ_p C_O ∇σ_n (Eq. 1, p. 225) and continuity Eqs. (2)-(3); Onsager multicomponent substitutional diffusion with Svoboda et al. kinetic coefficients (Eqs. 4-16, pp. 225-226), reduced to diagonal diffusivities in practice (Eq. 17, p. 226: "only the diagonal components in matrix D̄ are considered for simplicity"); chemical potentials from Thermo-Calc Gibbs energy g_m (Eqs. 9-11); a microstructure-explicit dislocation-based viscoplastic law for the γ' alloy (Eqs. 18-28); oxide fraction φ_O as order parameter with mixture stiffness and Pilling-Bedworth transformation strain (Eqs. 29-33, pp. 226-227). Solved with RBF-FD inside URDFIL (Eq. 34) coupled to Thermo-Calc TQ for local phase equilibrium (Sec. 3.2.2, p. 228). Oxide failure criterion Eqs. (35)-(36) (pp. 232-233).

**Oxygen transport.** Constant diffusivities and stress factor, calibrated to Kitaguchi et al. The oxide is a homogeneous continuum, p. 225: "The oxide is treated as a homogeneous continuum and effective mass transport parameters will be used to model the stress-assisted diffusion of ions within the oxide scale." p. 229: "for nodes within the alloy matrix, D11 = D22 = D_bulk and for nodes along the grain boundary, D11 = D_bulk and D22 = D_gb. Based on the observed thickness of oxide intrusion (∼200 nm) [12], D_bulk was estimated to be 2.22 × 10^-18 m^2/s [22,45]. ... In the present study, D_gb = 4 × 10^-16 m^2/s was used [48]." and "From TEM characterisation by Kitaguchi et al. [12], the oxide length ahead of the crack of a stress-free sample was found to be 9.7 µm after exposure of 5 h and from Fig. 6 this corresponds to a κ_p value of 3.85 × 10^-7 Pa^-1." No dependence of D_O on oxide phase, composition or porosity.

**Alloy/GB composition input; evolving oxide composition.** Yes, partially: concentration fields of Ni, Al, Cr, O evolve and local phases are determined thermodynamically, p. 228: "Only the two primary alloying elements, Al and Cr, are considered for simplicity instead of involving all elements in RR1000. At each time step, the upgraded composition information calculated through the RBF-FD method is passed to the Thermo-calc kernel and then a thermodynamic calculation based on the current composition is then performed for each point. The composition of each phase is calculated along with other variables needed for the calculations of g_m in Eqs. (10) and (11) and diffusion coefficients of chemical species." However only NiO is predicted, p. 230: "In the current simulations, formation of NiO was predicted along the grain boundary ... However, a discrepancy between the predicted and measured Ni concentrations can be observed in Fig. 8. This is attributed to the present simulations not accounting for the formation of chromium oxide, which results in a higher Ni concentration than would otherwise be expected." GB composition is the bulk alloy composition (the GB is a straight line with enhanced D_gb only, p. 227).

**Irradiation / RIS.** Not mentioned.

**Predictions and comparisons.** Wedge-shaped intrusion (Fig. 7) and Ni/Al/Cr/O profiles across the oxide compared with Kitaguchi et al. EDX (Fig. 8, p. 230); zero-stress growth called parabolic, p. 230: "Also included in the figure is the zero applied stress parabolic growth predictions. It can be seen that oxide formation is accelerated in the presence of an applied stress field and longer oxide intrusions are predicted as the applied stress increases." Compressive stress near the crack tip turning tensile at the oxide tip (Fig. 9, 11); oxide fracture after ~90-119 s with 0.42-0.45 µm oxide giving da/dt 3.8-4.7×10^-6 mm/s (Table 5, p. 233) compared with 1-3600-1-1 dwell data (Fig. 14, p. 234).

**Stated limitations.** p. 232: "Therefore, related experimental study is needed for a more precise understanding of the mechanism of crack tip oxide formation and for validation of the simulations presented here." p. 233: "The first of these is treating the oxide as a perfectly homogeneous material. However, as NiO has a porous microstructure [66], this may lead to a lower failure stress condition than that currently predicted ... The second simplifying assumption deals with the influence of prior deformation history."

**Relation to the manuscript.** The closest "composition in, oxide phase out" model via CALPHAD coupling, but oxygen transport is a constant anisotropic D unrelated to the oxide formed, and only NiO emerges; the manuscript's explicit multi-oxide D_eff and RIS-driven GB chemistry go beyond it.

## 5. zhao2011modeling

**Citation as printed.** L. G. Zhao, "Modeling of Oxygen Diffusion Along Grain Boundaries in a Nickel-Based Superalloy", Journal of Engineering Materials and Technology (Transactions of the ASME) Vol. 133, 031002, July 2011, doi 10.1115/1.4003777.

**Model type and governing equations.** Finite-element oxygen diffusion in a 150-grain Voronoi submodel of RR1000, GBs meshed as 1D diffusion elements, bulk diffusivity set negligible. Governing law Eq. (1) (p. 031002-2): ∂C/∂t = ∇·(D∇C − DCM∇P), with hydrostatic stress P and pressure factor M. Surface BC from parabolic mass gain (ΔW)^2 = κt (Eq. 2) giving flux J = ½(κ/t)^0.5 (Eq. 3). Mechanics: Chaboche viscoplasticity (Eqs. 4-5) globally and crystal plasticity (Eqs. 6-7) in the submodel, sequentially coupled.

**Oxygen transport.** Constant, temperature-dependent D fitted to surface scale thickness. p. 031002-2: "where C is the concentration of the solute, t is the time, ∇ is the gradient, D is the oxygen diffusivity, P is the hydrostatic stress (pressure), and M is the pressure factor." p. 031002-3: "In this paper, oxygen diffusion was assumed to occur along the grain boundaries only ... Oxygen diffusion in bulk material was excluded in the diffusion analyses by assigning negligible diffusivity to the bulk elements." p. 031002-3: "the oxygen diffusivity was calculated using the oxide scale thickness measured in Ref. [27]. The diffusivities were found to be 4.001 × 10^-14 mm^2/s, 2.222 × 10^-13 mm^2/s, 8.889 × 10^-13 mm^2/s, and 5.556 × 10^-12 mm^2/s for 650 °C, 700 °C, 750 °C, and 800 °C, respectively." No dependence on oxide phase, composition, porosity or alloy composition; there is no oxide in the model.

**Alloy/GB composition input; oxide composition.** None. p. 031002-4: "However, in the present work, no attempt was made to model chemical reaction kinetics, vacancy formation, or the transport of other species".

**Irradiation / RIS.** Not mentioned.

**Predictions and comparisons.** Oxygen penetration depth along GBs after 200 h: 2.2 µm (650 °C), ~2-3 µm (700 °C), 4.9 µm (750 °C), 8.8 µm (800 °C) (p. 031002-3); with 1100 MPa creep load at 750 °C depth 6.7 µm = 1.4 × natural (p. 031002-4); pressure factors 6.03×10^-3 to 0.961 mm^2/N estimated from Karabela et al. damage depths (p. 031002-4). Oxygen accumulation at a 5.2 µm intergranular crack tip (Sec. 5.3). Comparison is to Encinas-Oropesa TGA/FIB scale thickness and Karabela waisted-specimen damage, qualitatively (pp. 031002-3 to -5).

**Stated limitations.** p. 031002-4 (quoted above, no chemistry); p. 031002-6: "special grain boundaries with a high fraction of coincident lattice sites exhibit a higher resistance to oxidation attack when compared with random high-angle grain boundaries [26]. This aspect has not been considered in the present work and needs to be explored in further work." and "surface microcracks will introduce both stress gradient and curvature gradient, and the latter was not considered".

**Relation to the manuscript.** A pure constant-D Fickian GB oxygen transport model (depth ∝ √t by construction of the BC) with no oxide chemistry; it represents the "constant D_GB" class that the manuscript's phase-dependent D_eff is meant to supersede.

## 6. karabela2013oxygen

**Citation as printed.** A. Karabela, L.G. Zhao, B. Lin, J. Tong, M.C. Hardy, "Oxygen diffusion and crack growth for a nickel-based superalloy under fatigue-oxidation conditions", Materials Science & Engineering A 567 (2013) 46-57, doi 10.1016/j.msea.2012.12.088.

**Model type and governing equations.** Same FE framework as Zhao 2011: Eq. (1) (p. 48) ∂C/∂t = ∇·(D∇C − DCM∇P); parabolic flux BC Eqs. (2)-(3) (p. 48); Chaboche viscoplasticity Eqs. (4)-(8) and crystal plasticity Eqs. (9)-(12) (pp. 48-49); diffusivity extracted from FIB internal-oxidation depth via Eq. (13) (p. 52): X^2 = Dt. New: FIB measurements on waisted specimens, calibration of D and M, and a fatigue-oxidation failure curve (accumulated inelastic strain vs oxygen concentration) for CT crack growth prediction (Sec. 8).

**Oxygen transport.** Constant D per temperature from an explicit parabolic law. p. 52: "The diffusivity (or diffusion coefficient) was calculated based on [19] X^2 = Dt (13) where D is the diffusivity, X is the maximum depth of internal oxidation measured from the FIB analysis in Section 3 (position C for the waisted specimen) and t is the time." Table 3 (p. 52): D = 1.085×10^-13, 1.397×10^-12, 5.859×10^-12, 1.423×10^-11 mm^2/s at 650, 700, 750, 800 °C. Pressure factor Table 4 (p. 53): 0.001585-0.188 MPa^-1. No dependence on oxide phase, composition, porosity or alloy composition. The authors acknowledge that the measured quantity is oxide depth, p. 55: "It should be noted that the FIB measurements do not really give the depth of oxygen diffusion but the depth of oxide. The depth of oxygen diffusion could be larger than the depth of oxide since oxygen can further penetrate along the grain boundaries without the formation of oxides. This work also assumes that the depth of oxygen diffusion follows a power law with a time exponent of 1/2 (Eq. (13)), as opposed to a time exponent of 1/4 suggested in Reuchet and Remy [61] and Gustafsson et al. [62]."

**Alloy/GB composition input; oxide composition.** None in the model. Oxide chemistry only discussed, p. 54: "Internal oxide particles are considered to be a mixture of TiO2 and Al2O3 [28,58,59]."

**Irradiation / RIS.** Not mentioned.

**Predictions and comparisons.** FIB: internal oxidation depth up to 1.0 µm (700 °C), 2.5 µm (750 °C), 7.0 µm (800 °C) at the highest-stress location; stress effect notable at 750-800 °C, negligible at 700 °C (pp. 51-52, Fig. 7). FE at 800 °C with M = 0.3 MPa^-1: depth 7.313 µm vs 3.207 µm natural (p. 52). Crack growth rates vs frequency and dwell at ΔK = 30, 40 MPa√m at 650 °C compared with CT data (Figs. 11-12, pp. 54-55); model "underestimates the crack growth rate for long-dwells (typically >300 s, Fig. 12)" (p. 56).

**Stated limitations.** p. 56: "the determination of diffusion parameters was based on the measured depth of oxides, rather than the depth of oxygen diffusion, using a parabolic law. This could give an underestimation of the diffusivity and the pressure factor". p. 56: "diffusion of oxygen was modelled using a simplified approach, which considers only the influence of stress on diffusion without the full interaction between deformation and oxygen diffusion. Consequently, the growth stresses (may reach 1 GPa [68] and contribute to crack growth) induced by oxidation itself was disregarded in our model." p. 55: "So far, no direct measurements are available for oxygen diffusion parameters in nickel alloys."

**Relation to the manuscript.** Explicitly adopts X^2 = Dt with a constant D fitted to oxide depth, i.e. exactly case (a); it has no oxide phase dependence or alloy chemistry.

## 7. kitaguchi2013oxidation

**Citation as printed.** H.S. Kitaguchi, H.Y. Li, H.E. Evans, R.G. Ding, I.P. Jones, G. Baxter, P. Bowen, "Oxidation ahead of a crack tip in an advanced Ni-based superalloy", Acta Materialia 61 (2013) 1968-1981, doi 10.1016/j.actamat.2012.12.017.

**Model type and governing equations.** Primarily experimental (FIB plan-view TEM/EDX of RR1000 crack-tip intrusions at 650 °C). The only equations are thermodynamic: Eq. (1) xM + O2 → M_xO2, Eq. (2) K_eq = a_MxO2/(a_M^x p_O2) = exp(−ΔG°/RT), Eq. (3) dissociation pressure p* = (a_MxO2/a_M^x) exp(ΔG°/RT) (p. 1977), used to map p(O2) across (Fig. 15) and along (Fig. 16) the intrusion from the observed oxide layering.

**Oxygen transport.** No diffusivity or kinetic law is used. Oxygen access is argued to depend on the oxide type at the intrusion tip: p. 1979: "For both Sample B and Sample C, the sealing of the tip of the oxide intrusion with an alumina layer implies that negligible quantities of oxygen would exist in solution in the alloy ahead of the intrusion." p. 1980 (Conclusion 3): "It is argued that the oxygen diffusion flux through such protective layers will also be small and that the oxygen that crosses will be readily gettered by reactive elements (Al, Cr, Ti) within the alloy." The paper also notes, p. 1969, that long-range oxygen transport "at the relatively low temperatures of interest (e.g. 650 °C) are simply not credible [13] unless substantial stress-aided diffusion processes are present."

**Alloy/GB composition input; oxide composition.** Alloy composition enters only as solute activities, p. 1977: "For the present purposes, it will be assumed that the activity, a_M, of each reacting solute is equal to its atom fraction within the alloy." (Table 2: Co 0.179, Ni 0.509, Cr 0.165, Ti 0.043, Al 0.064; p* at 923 K: CoO 4.18×10^-18, NiO 2.51×10^-18, Cr2O3 7.78×10^-33, TiO2 4.89×10^-41, Al2O3 1.05×10^-51 atm). Oxide composition is measured, not predicted: layered CoO/NiO core, Cr2O3, TiO2, Al2O3 adjacent to alloy (pp. 1971, 1979). Cr depletion ahead of/around the intrusion is observed, p. 1971-1972: "Ahead of the intrusion tip in this sample there appears to be depletion of Cr, presumably into the oxide forming the intrusion, and a corresponding enhancement of Ni." and p. 1974: "a dealloyed zone of a width of 50 nm, with lower Cr and higher Ni concentration, exists between the oxides and the matrix." The healing mechanism is tied to GB Cr supply, p. 1979: "This healing will arise principally through the supply of chromium to the oxidation front via the alloy grain boundary ahead of the intrusion. ... Whether or not a complete chromia layer will form at the NiO intrusion tip will depend on the rate of supply of chromium and the width of the pre-existing oxide intrusion."

**Irradiation / RIS.** Not mentioned.

**Predictions and comparisons.** Data rather than predictions: intrusion 1.5 µm ahead of a crack growing at 6 µm/s (p. 1971); 4.6 µm after 5 h at K = 0.9K_th (p. 1973); 11.2 µm total, +9.7 µm during 5 h at K ≈ 0 (p. 1978); NiO intrusion in sample C eventually "healed at the intrusion tip through the formation of chromia and alumina layers" (p. 1979); p(O2) drops from ~10^-18 to ~10^-51 atm within the last ~200 nm of the sample-B intrusion (Fig. 16, p. 1980).

**Stated limitations.** p. 1977: "This assumption may lead to uncertainty in the actual calculated values of oxygen partial pressure but is not expected to affect significantly the predicted trends in its spatial variation within the intrusion." p. 1979: "The reason why chromia is the main constituent in the oxide intrusion of Sample B, but is not the main constituent in Sample C, can be understood in principle but not in detail."

**Relation to the manuscript.** Provides the experimental rationale (oxide layering, protective vs non-protective tip oxide, Cr supply along the GB controlling healing) for making D_eff depend on which oxide is present at the front, but offers no kinetic model.

## 8. gellings1967grain

**Citation as printed.** P. J. Gellings and M. A. de Jongh, "Grain Boundary Oxidation and the Chromium-Depletion Theory of Intercrystalline Corrosion of Austenitic Stainless Steels", Corrosion Science, 1967, Vol. 7, pp. 413-421 (Technische Hogeschool Twente, Enschede).

**Model type and governing equations.** High-temperature microscopy of AISI 304/347/321/316 oxidised at 650 °C in residual H2O/O2 in argon, plus an Appendix (pp. 419-421) solving Fick's second law for Cr (D1) and C (D2) perpendicular to a GB plane x = 0 during Cr23C6 precipitation (reaction 4Cr + C → Cr4C simplified), with flux coupling D1 ∂C1/∂x = 4D2 ∂C2/∂x at x = 0, replaced by the approximation C1 = 0 at x = 0 (Eq. 6, p. 420). Laplace-transform solution gives Cr profile C1 = C1° erfc[x/2√(D1 t)] (Eq. 10, p. 421) and the carbon profile Eq. (11).

**Oxygen transport.** None; oxygen diffusivity does not appear. The oxidation rate dependence on Cr is empirical (Fig. 1, p. 415): "The chromium-depletion theory, however, predicts that the oxidation rate at high temperatures will also be greater along the grain boundaries due to the precipitation of chromium carbide which thus lowers the Cr content of the alloy at these areas. Figure 1 shows how the rate of oxidation increases with decreasing chromium-content."

**Alloy/GB composition input; oxide composition.** Alloy Cr (18 %) and C content are inputs; the output is the width of the Cr-depleted zone, not an oxide. p. 421: "From the literature we have at T = 923 °K D1 = 2 × 10^-13 cm^2 sec^-1, D2 = 2 × 10^-9 cm^2 sec^-1 ... If we assume that at 12 % Cr the oxidation is noticeably faster (see Fig. 1) we have erfc[x/2√(D1 t)] = 0.67 which means x/2√(D1 t) = 0.7. After an annealing time of 20 h this gives for the expected width of the oxidized zone x = 0.7 × 2 × √(2 × 10^-13 × 7.2 × 10^4) = 1.67 × 10^-4 cm = 1.67 µ". No oxide composition is predicted; oxide thickness is inferred from interference colours (p. 417).

**Irradiation / RIS.** Not mentioned; Cr depletion is by thermal carbide sensitisation (heat treatments a-f, p. 416).

**Predictions and comparisons.** p. 418: "The measurements show that after about 7 min the width of the oxidized zone is approximately 2 µ and in the Appendix a derivation is given for the width of the depleted zone (which is of course not sharply defined). This calculation gives 1.67 µ for the width which is in good accord with the experimental result." Qualitative classification of GB oxidation / general / intermediate behaviour vs heat treatment (Table 2, p. 417).

**Stated limitations.** p. 420: "where it is implicitly assumed that the reaction is fast compared to the mass transport by diffusion"; the equilibrium condition "leads to a non-linear problem and therefore we take as an approximation at the boundary C1 = 0"; p. 421 the approximation is valid only if %C ≫ 0.015 %.

**Relation to the manuscript.** Historically the first explicit link between a lateral GB Cr-depletion profile (erfc law) and locally enhanced GB oxidation in 304 SS; it is the thermal-sensitisation analogue of the manuscript's RIS-depleted GB, but contains no oxygen transport or oxide model.

## 9. huang2022multilayer

**Citation as printed.** Shu Huang, Ryan Kerr, Samuel Murphy, Mark R Gilbert and Jaime Marian, "Multilayer interface tracking model of pure tungsten oxidation", Modelling and Simulation in Materials Science and Engineering 30 (2022) 085015 (25 pp), doi 10.1088/1361-651X/aca111.

**Model type and governing equations.** 1D sharp-interface (Stefan) multilayer model of a planar scale on pure W with sublayers WO3 / WO2.9 / WO2.72 / WO2 / W-O metal. Surface oxygen pick-up Eq. (4) (p. 4); interface velocity from the flux jump Eq. (5) (p. 5) ṡ_i = ΔJ_i|s_i/(n_i ρ_W); Fick's laws Eqs. (6)-(7) in each layer with fixed free-oxygen concentrations at each interface (Table 1; layer-by-layer equations pp. 7-8); explicit finite differences Eqs. (8)-(9) (δx = 1 nm, δt = 1 ns); Arrhenius diffusivities Eq. (10), WO2 diffusivity from DFT vacancy formation/migration Eq. (11) and (C.6); Stefan derivation Appendix B Eq. (B.2); DFT+U Appendix C.

**Oxygen transport.** A separate constant Arrhenius diffusivity for each oxide phase (Table 2, p. 11): WO3 D0 = 6.8×10^-6 m^2/s, 1.3 eV; WO2.9 1.40 eV; WO2.72 1.22 eV; WO2 3.1×10^-5 m^2/s, 1.90 eV; W metal 7.6×10^-8 m^2/s, 0.15 eV. p. 9: "That leaves the diffusivities D_i (i = 1,5) as the remaining material constants to determine." Transport therefore depends on the oxide phase, and cracked layers become short circuits, p. 8: "When a layer i bounded by oxygen concentrations of c_i−1 and c_i fragments after it reaches a critical width, w*_i, it opens up percolation pathways that equalize the free oxygen concentration across it [33], i.e. c_i−1 = c_i. When such a situation arises, the layer in question stops growing and simply acts as an oxygen bridge between its two adjacent layers." No porosity or composition continuum variable; no GB.

**Alloy/GB composition input; oxide composition.** Pure W only; no alloy composition. Oxide stoichiometry sequence is prescribed from the phase diagram and controlled by the surface concentration c0, p. 15: "we control the formation of the different oxides simply by regulating the amount of available oxygen, c0, on the environment side of the exposed metal. c0 is in fact the only parameter of our model that is 'adjusted' to reproduce certain observations." Layer thicknesses (hence scale composition) evolve in time.

**Irradiation / RIS.** Not in the model; irradiation appears only as context (fusion LOCA, "radioactive oxide", p. 2).

**Predictions and comparisons.** Non-parabolic exponents: p. 12: "A power law fit to the s4-t graph yields s4(t) = 3.747 t^0.407, indicating a substantial deviation from pure parabolic growth law." Table 3 (p. 14): exponents b = 0.407 (873 K, 2 sublayers), 0.413 (923 K, 4), 0.441 (973 K, 2), 0.454 (1073 K, 1), 0.490 (1273 K, 1). Conclusion (d), p. 17: "The time evolution of the oxide scale matches parabolic growth only at high temperatures and when only one oxide sublayer is involved. Whenever multiple layers are at play, measurable deviations from parabolic growth can be appreciated." Effective k_p compared with Habainy, Gulbransen, Cifuentes, Sikka data (Fig. 6, p. 16): "the simulated values generally overestimate the experimental measurements, they lie within the broader range of the data at some temperatures."

**Stated limitations.** p. 5: "here we ignore the relative volumetric expansion of the oxide layers relative to the initial metal phase". p. 15: "fracture and fragmentation is not a part of the current model". p. 15: "sublimation is not a feature of the present model". p. 6: assumes excess oxygen above stoichiometry is insoluble and that the build-up time "does not compute towards the total oxide layer formation time". p. 23: DFT diffusivities not computed under the compressive stress the oxide actually experiences.

**Relation to the manuscript.** The one paper in the set in which oxygen transport is explicitly phase-dependent (one D per oxide) and in which multi-phase scales are shown to produce non-parabolic depth-time laws; but it is a planar pure-metal scale with no GB, no alloy composition and no composition-dependent reaction, so the manuscript's GB/alloy/RIS coupling is complementary rather than duplicated.

## 11. persaud2016review

**Citation as printed.** S.Y. Persaud and R.C. Newman, "A Review of Oxidation Phenomena in Ni Alloys Exposed to Hydrogenated Steam Below 500°C", Corrosion, Vol. 72, No. 7 (July 2016), pp. 881-896, doi 10.5006/1957 (NACE International).

## Cross-cutting answers to the three questions

**(a) Constant GB oxygen diffusivity with an analytical front law.** Xu 2015 (single D_o = 10^-6 cm^2/s; semi-analytical v_s(L_s) from Eqs. 31-33; quasi-constant velocity), Evans 2013 (h = (k_p t)^0.5 with literature k_p; S_σ multiplier), Zhao 2011 and Karabela 2013 (constant D per temperature; Karabela explicitly X^2 = Dt). Gellings 1967 gives an analytical erfc law but for the Cr-depletion width, with no oxygen diffusivity.

**(b) Oxygen transport dependent on the oxide already formed.** Only Huang 2022 implements it (one constant D per oxide sublayer, cracked layers as oxygen bridges), and it is for a planar scale on pure tungsten. Sushko 2015 predicts oxide porosity and argues it controls O access but does not use a continuum D. Kitaguchi 2013 and Persaud & Newman 2016 argue it experimentally (sealing Cr2O3/Al2O3 vs non-protective NiO; Cr-rich vs porous Fe3O4). Evans 2013 varies k_p and Φ by oxide type but assumes one oxide per intrusion. Fang 2022, Zhao 2011 and Karabela 2013 use a single D_O independent of the oxide.

**(c) Alloy/GB composition as input with evolving oxide composition.** Fang 2022 is the only continuum model that evolves Ni/Al/Cr/O fields and determines phases via Thermo-Calc, but it predicts only NiO and uses the bulk alloy composition at the GB. Xu 2015 takes bulk composition (explicitly assuming no GB segregation) and predicts oxide A/B/O mole fractions without feedback on transport. Sushko 2015 takes alloy composition and GB-type barriers and predicts element partitioning in a prescribed oxide phase at steady state. No paper takes an RIS-modified (or any dose-dependent) GB composition as input; irradiation appears only in Yu 2021, which is not an oxidation model.
